A Unified Computational Ontology for the Relativistic Scalar–Vector Plenum

Introduction: The Quest for a Computational Substrate

Cognitive science has long been animated by the fundamental question of what constitutes thought itself, separate from its sensory manifestations. The phenomena of aphantasia—the absence of voluntary mental imagery—and anendophasia—the absence of a conversational inner voice—present a profound challenge to traditional models that presuppose phenomenological content as a prerequisite for cognition. These conditions reveal that complex reasoning, planning, and creativity can persist in the absence of internal pictures or words, suggesting the existence of a more primitive, amodal computational substrate.

The Relativistic Scalar–Vector Plenum (RSVP) was developed as a field-theoretic model to formalize this mode of cognition, describing thought as the direct traversal of a semantic geometry. This monograph advances a central thesis: that the RSVP framework can be grounded in a unified computational foundation, and that this foundation must be a specific three-layer hierarchy to solve a deep problem in computational neuroscience—the explanatory gap between abstract, rule-based computation and the physical dynamics of neural fields. We will establish this by demonstrating the formal equivalence and hierarchical relationship between three distinct interpretative layers: a symbolic linear λ–calculus providing an abstract operational semantics, a geometric SpherePop calculus discretizing the plenum into interacting microstates, and a statistical 5D Ising model describing the emergence of cognitive coherence through thermodynamic synchronization.

This treatise is structured to guide the reader from the macroscopic to the microscopic and back to a unified whole. Part I will lay out the foundational principles of the classical RSVP field theory. Part II will provide a detailed exposition of each of the three computational layers—symbolic, geometric, and statistical—analyzing their internal dynamics and cognitive interpretations. Part III will achieve the monograph's ultimate goal: a formal synthesis of these layers using the languages of category and sheaf theory, proving their structural integrity and operational equivalence. We will conclude by considering the broader implications of this unified computational ontology for understanding neurodiversity, designing artificial intelligence, and reformulating core questions in the philosophy of mind. This investigation begins with the foundational principles of the RSVP framework, where cognition is first understood as a continuous process of entropic smoothing across a semantic field.

Part I: Foundations of the RSVP Framework

1. The Classical RSVP Field Theory: Cognition as Entropic Smoothing

To construct a computational hierarchy, we must first define the macroscopic target that the microscopic layers aim to describe. The classical field theory of the Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum (RSVP) serves precisely this role. It provides a continuous, deterministic description of cognitive dynamics at a large scale, framing thought as an entropic relaxation process within a geometric manifold. This chapter lays out the core principles and mathematical formalism of the RSVP as a model of wordless, imageless thought, establishing the physical-level phenomena that our subsequent computational models must reproduce.

* Scalar Potential (Φ): This field represents the intensity or concentration of semantic potential at a given point in the cognitive manifold. High values of Φ correspond to regions of significant meaning or conceptual focus.
* Vector Flow (v): This field represents the directed currents of cognitive activity, such as the flow of attention or the trajectory of an inferential process. It advects the scalar potential, guiding the evolution of semantic focus across the manifold.
* Entropy Density (S): This field represents the local configurational complexity or informational uncertainty. It modulates the dynamics of the other fields and evolves based on the structure emerging from their interactions.

This system formalizes cognition as a process of entropic smoothing. Thought is not a discrete sequence of logical steps but a continuous field relaxation. The flow of attention is guided by gradients of meaning, and the very structure of this interaction generates local information (entropy), which then diffuses, driving the system toward states of greater coherence and lower potential energy.

While these PDEs provide a powerful description of the macroscopic dynamics of imageless thought, they are silent on the underlying computational substrate. They describe what happens but not how these dynamics are implemented at a more fundamental level. This ambiguity motivates the central inquiry of this monograph: the need for micro-level models to explain how the continuous, entropic smoothing of the RSVP field is realized. This quest leads us to explore the computational hierarchy that underpins the plenum.

Part II: The Computational Hierarchy

The classical RSVP field theory specifies the target dynamics of wordless thought, but the central question of implementation remains. To bridge the gap between this continuous, macroscopic description and a physically plausible micro-level substrate, we propose a three-layer computational hierarchy. 2. The Symbolic Layer: RSVP as a Linear λ–Calculus

To understand the computational engine driving the RSVP field, it is invaluable to interpret its dynamics through the formal lens of computation theory. The linear λ–calculus provides the most abstract, symbolic layer in our proposed hierarchy. This formalism recasts cognitive dynamics not as the flow of continuous fields, but as a discrete sequence of rule-based transformations on symbolic terms. It provides a rigorous operational semantics for cognitive computation, where each step corresponds to a thermodynamically consistent act of inference.

The syntax of the RSVP λ–calculus is built upon four base types, each corresponding to a fundamental component of the cognitive plenum:

* Φ: Represents the scalar potential field.
* v: Represents the vector flow field.
* S: Represents the entropy field.
* Ω: Represents the awareness classifier, a predicate encoding conscious accessibility.

Terms in this calculus denote either local field configurations or the transformations that act upon them. The linear nature of the calculus, enforced by its typing rules, ensures that cognitive resources are conserved—each field component is used exactly once in any given transformation, mirroring the conservation laws of the physical system.

The central computational mechanic of this layer is β-reduction, the fundamental rule of substitution in the λ–calculus: (λx.t)u → t[x := u]. In the RSVP context, this abstract rule acquires a profound physical and cognitive meaning. Each step of β-reduction corresponds to a transformation of the cognitive state that results in a non-increasing value for a local entropy functional. Computation, therefore, is a form of thermodynamic inference. Every logical step of substitution is simultaneously an act of local entropic smoothing, driving the system toward a state of lower free energy and greater order.

This direct correspondence between symbolic manipulation and physical dynamics is formalized in the Principle of Field–Computational Equivalence. This principle asserts that the stationary points of the system's action functional, when evolved through sequences of β-reductions, coincide with the solutions to the RSVP Euler-Lagrange equations derived from the continuous field theory. In essence, the logical fixed points of the computation are identical to the physical equilibrium points of the field. This powerful result unifies the symbolic-computational description with the physical-field description, demonstrating that they are two languages describing the same underlying process.

The linear λ–calculus thus serves as a powerful but abstract symbolic engine for RSVP cognition. It defines the "software" of thought in terms of pure, rule-based transformation. Yet, a formal calculus is insufficient on its own to ground a physical theory of mind. It lacks intrinsic notions of spatiality, adjacency, and locality that are fundamental to any field-theoretic model. This raises the critical question of implementation: how might such formal rules be realized in a substrate with a geometric structure? This inquiry leads us from the symbolic to the geometric layer of the hierarchy, the SpherePop calculus.

3. The Geometric Layer: RSVP as the SpherePop Calculus

The SpherePop calculus offers a geometric discretization of the continuous RSVP plenum, providing a crucial bridge between the abstract symbolic layer and the statistical-mechanical layer. It models cognitive dynamics not as continuous fields or symbolic reductions, but as the collective behavior of discrete microstates on a finite lattice. This perspective recasts the smooth, flowing landscape of the classical theory into a granular space of local configurations whose interactions give rise to the macroscopic field.

At the heart of this layer is the concept of a Sphere Configuration. The cognitive manifold is represented as a finite lattice, and each site i on this lattice is occupied by a "sphere," which is a microstate bundle (Φi, vi, Si). Each sphere encapsulates the local state of the plenum, representing the semantic charge (Φi), attentional flow (vi), and informational entropy (Si) within a bounded cognitive patch. These spheres are not isolated; they interact with their neighbors, creating a dynamic system of coupled microstates.

The core dynamic of this layer is the "Popping Rule." A pop is a local update event at a single site i where the sphere's configuration changes to a new state σ'i such that the local potential energy is reduced (U'i < Ui). This rule is fundamentally a principle of local relaxation. A sequence of such pops across the lattice realizes a discrete form of entropy descent. Each pop is a micro-reorganization that smooths out local inconsistencies and reduces potential energy, mirroring on a microscopic scale the global entropic smoothing described by the continuous RSVP field equations.

The critical theoretical result of this model is its formal convergence to the macroscopic field theory. In the continuum limit, as the lattice spacing h approaches zero, the collective dynamics governed by the popping rule converge to the continuous RSVP field equations. This mathematical proof establishes the SpherePop calculus as a legitimate microscopic model for the macroscopic field dynamics. It demonstrates that the smooth, flowing cognition of the classical theory can emerge from the discrete, local, and mechanistic interactions of countless microstates.

The SpherePop calculus thus provides a deterministic, geometric model of microstate relaxation. It populates the abstract plenum with concrete computational entities and defines their behavior with a simple, energy-minimizing rule. However, the deterministic popping of individual spheres is inadequate for explaining emergent, collective phenomena such as large-scale cognitive coherence or the phase transitions between different mental states. To describe the statistical behavior of a vast ensemble of such spheres and understand how global order arises from local interactions, we must move to the final layer of the hierarchy: the statistical-mechanical interpretation provided by the 5D Ising model.

Part III: Formal Synthesis and Integration

5. A Categorical and Sheaf-Theoretic Unification

This chapter serves as the theoretical capstone of the monograph. Its purpose is to formally synthesize the three preceding computational layers—symbolic, geometric, and statistical—into a single, coherent mathematical structure. By employing the powerful and abstract tools of category theory and sheaf theory, we can move beyond mere analogy and provide a rigorous proof of their hierarchical structure and formal equivalence, thereby demonstrating the central thesis of this work. This synthesis reveals a deep, underlying unity where computation, geometry, and thermodynamics are not just related, but are different perspectives on the same fundamental process.

The unification begins by defining three distinct symmetric monoidal categories, one for each computational layer:

* Cλ: The category of the λ–calculus, where objects are well-typed linear λ-terms and morphisms are energy-non-increasing reduction steps.
* CSp: The category of the SpherePop calculus, where objects are SpherePop microstate bundles and morphisms are energy-decreasing popping sequences.
* CIs: The category of the Ising model, where objects are 5D Ising configurations and morphisms are synchronization steps that lower the Hamiltonian.

With these categories established, we can formalize the relationships between them as "Functorial Bridges." These are energy-monotone functors, Fλ→Sp and FSp→Is, that act as compilers, translating structures from one layer to the next. The significance of these functors is profound: they provide a formal mapping showing how symbolic β-reductions in the λ–calculus are compiled into geometric SpherePop pops, which are in turn compiled into Ising spin updates on the lattice. Crucially, as formalized in Theorem 16.8, these functors are energy-monotone, meaning that energy or entropy decreases (or stays the same) at each step of the compilation. This guarantees that a process of simplification in the symbolic realm corresponds directly to a process of physical relaxation in the geometric and statistical realms.

Building on this categorical structure, we introduce a sheaf-theoretic perspective to explain how local computations are integrated into a global, coherent cognitive state. By assigning a local computational category C(U) (containing objects and morphisms from all three layers) to an open set U of the RSVP manifold, we define a presheaf. The cognitive meaning of the "Sheaf Condition" is that if local cognitive processes agree on their overlaps, they can be uniquely "glued" together to form a unified, consistent global state. This mathematical operation is the formal equivalent of synchronizing overlapping cognitive subsystems—such as vision and motor planning—into a single, coherent experience or belief.

This perspective gives us a powerful tool to quantify cognitive inconsistency. The concept of "Semantic Holonomy" arises from the first Čech cohomology group, Ȟ1(M, C), which measures the obstructions to this gluing process. A nontrivial cohomology class corresponds to a situation where local beliefs or representations are consistent in pairs but form a globally contradictory loop. Cognitively, this represents a "cognitive dissonance loop"—a set of irreconcilable beliefs that prevents the formation of a single, coherent global state and reveals a "twist" in the conceptual manifold.

This categorical and sheaf-theoretic integration provides the definitive proof of a unified, multi-layered computational ontology for the RSVP framework. It demonstrates that the symbolic, geometric, and statistical descriptions are not just metaphors for one another but are rigorously connected levels of a single, hierarchical computational system.

Conclusion: A Unified Field Theory of Computation in Cognition

This monograph has pursued a central argument: that the Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum (RSVP), a field-theoretic model of cognition, can be grounded in a coherent and formally unified computational ontology. We have demonstrated this by constructing and integrating a three-tiered hierarchy of computational layers: a symbolic linear λ–calculus, a geometric SpherePop calculus, and a statistical 5D Ising model. This synthesis provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for understanding the mechanics of thought, particularly in its most abstract, amodal forms.

The layered architecture we have detailed offers a compelling explanation for wordless, imageless thought. Aphantasia and anendophasia are reframed not as deficits, but as natural computational regimes within the RSVP framework. In these modes, the underlying semantic dynamics—the evolution of potential, flow, and entropy—persist with full computational integrity, but without being projected onto sensory cortices for phenomenological rendering. Cognition, in its purest form, is the evolution of the field itself. The key result of this work is the formal synthesis from our final part, where functorial bridges and sheaf-theoretic gluing provide a rigorous mathematical proof of the equivalence and hierarchical relationship between the layers. This integration is more than a technical achievement; it unifies the core principles of computation (rule-based transformation), geometry (spatial relaxation), and thermodynamics (entropic descent) within a single, cohesive model of cognition.

It is crucial, however, to frame the classical theory presented herein as the necessary foundation upon which the next frontiers of research must be built. The entire formulation has deliberately omitted quantum effects to first establish a stable classical basis. A significant avenue for future work is the development of a quantum-field treatment of the RSVP plenum, which would involve replacing classical fields with operator algebras, exploring the interference of semantic phases, and investigating the role of decoherence in cognitive processes. Furthermore, while this monograph provides a complete theoretical framework, the essential next steps are empirical calibration and computational implementation. Building simulation pipelines and validating the model's predictions against neurophysiological data will be necessary to bridge this theory with experimental cognitive science.

Ultimately, this monograph offers a vision of the mind as a self-relaxing, entropic field. It recasts thought as the continuous smoothing of informational gradients, a process governed by universal principles that echo across physics, computation, and biology. In this view, consciousness is not a mysterious ghost in the machine but an emergent property of informational coherence—a stable, synchronized state that arises when the underlying field finds a momentary equilibrium. The framework presented here is a testament to the idea that the deepest questions about the nature of mind may not be answered by biology or philosophy alone, but through a synthesis that recognizes thought itself as a fundamental physical and computational process, awaiting its full expression in the language of mathematics.
