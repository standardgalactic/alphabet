Modern medicine currently founders upon a "Projection Error"—a fundamental ontological category mismatch. By restricting clinical descriptions to physicochemical reductionism—tracking local state transitions, molecular concentrations, and reaction chains—we inadvertently discard the global topological invariants that define life.

This representational failure is formalized in **Proposition 1.1: Non-Invariance of Closure under Projection**.

Let G be a directed graph of processes containing a non-trivial cycle, and let π be any projection to a state-evolution representation recording only local transitions. The projection π(G) does not, in general, preserve the existence of the cycle as an invariant.

In clinical terms, a description can be locally accurate at every molecular coordinate yet globally blind to the "closure" that distinguishes a living organism from a non-living chemical soup.

The strategic mandate for 21st-century medicine is the transition from a state-evolution view to a closure-sensitive view. We must replace the standard particle-force ontology with the RSVP field-theoretic ontology (φ, v, S), where entropy (S) is locally regulated as an inversion of the thermodynamic gradient rather than globally maximized.

The fundamental unit of health is the **Admissible Endomorphism Loop (L(C) ≈ C)**.

* **Strong Closure (Robustness):**
  The system admits an endomorphism loop with a negative uncertainty gradient (dU/dt < 0). Under perturbation, the system actively returns to its viable constraint configuration.

* **Weak Closure (Fragility):**
  The system exists at the boundary of viability. It admits a loop in equilibrium but lacks the recursive self-enabling power to recover from significant displacements.

### The Sheaf of Autonomy

Life persists across biological scales:

cellular ≺ tissue ≺ organ ≺ organism

via the **Restriction Map (ρᵧₓ)**.

This map dictates how higher-order closures constrain lower-order ones.

Health is defined as **Admissible Gluing**:

* the lower level preserves its autonomy (internal endomorphism loop)
* its outputs contribute to the higher-level closure
* the higher level’s constraints do not eliminate essential loops of the lower level

Coherence across these maps constitutes the **global section** of the patient.

Pathology is the mathematical failure of this gluing.

## 3. Typology of Pathology: The Three Modes of Topological Failure

Pathology is not merely "damage" to matter; it is a failure of mathematical gluing and admissibility within the closure sheaf.

We identify three distinct modes of failure that require differentiated therapeutic strategies.

---

### Mode I: Loop Rupture

* **Formal Definition:** Stalk Lᵢ goes empty; total loss of the endomorphism loop.
* **Clinical Profile:** Localized cessation of self-maintenance; the metabolic cycle fails to regenerate enabling conditions.
* **Biological Exemplar:** Acute injury, localized cell death, or necrotic tissue.

---

### Mode II: Restriction Failure

* **Formal Definition:** Admissibility failure in the restriction map ρᵧₓ; the decoupling of levels.
* **Clinical Profile:** Lower-level loops persist but are no longer "glued" to the organismic closure.
* **Biological Exemplar:** Cancer. The malignant cell optimizes its own endomorphism loop but treats the organism as an external environment to exploit rather than a global section to maintain. The restriction map ρ₍organism, cell₎ fails.

---

### Mode III: Obstruction Accumulation

* **Formal Definition:** Growth of the H¹(X, 𝓕) cohomological obstruction class.
* **Clinical Profile:** Systemic failure where local stalks appear viable in isolation but fail to cohere into a unified global section.
* **Biological Exemplar:** Systemic aging, chronic inflammation, and multi-system failure.

---

### The Metric of Health: Degree of Partial Admissibility (α)

We mandate the evaluation of the degree of partial admissibility:

α ∈ [0, 1]

where α represents the fraction of essential endomorphism loops preserved by the restriction map ρᵧₓ.

* α = 1 → Full Admissibility (organismic health)
* 0 < α < 1 → Partial Admissibility (compensated pathology; latent vulnerability and senescence)
* α = 0 → Complete Restriction Failure (systemic collapse)

### Hierarchy of Intervention: Strategic Commands

Researchers must move beyond simple inhibition and develop the following therapeutic classes:

1. **Mandate Loop Restoration (Mode I):**
   Develop protocols for supplying missing constraints directly—such as exogenous metabolic stabilizers or bio-synthetic scaffolds—to allow ruptured endomorphism loops to resume.

2. **Implement Re-Gluing Operations (Mode II):**
   Shift focus from excision to the restoration of admissibility in broken restriction maps. This requires modulating the signaling environment, which constitutes the constraint structure mediating cellular and organismic levels (e.g., immunotherapies that re-couple cellular behavior to organismic regulation).

3. **Calibrate Obstruction Reduction (Mode III):**
   Deploy systemic field-space alterations to drive the descent of E_obs. This involves altering the global admissibility landscape to facilitate the re-emergence of a global section.

Traditional medicine excises (removing the failed stalk); topological medicine re-glues (restoring the restriction map ρᵧₓ).

We treat the patient not as a sum of parts, but as the existence of a global section.
