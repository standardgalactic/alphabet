The paper titled "Heave, Sway, and Surge: A Triaxial Theory of Constraint-Limited Cognition" proposes a unified framework to understand cognitive processes across various domains. This framework is based on three interconnected axes: Compression (Heave), Origin (Sway), and Temporal Binding (Surge).

1. **Axis I – Compression (Heave):**

   - The Heave axis represents the process of latent representation, akin to an information bottleneck. It's denoted by the equation f(x;θ) = z, where 'z' is a compressed representation of input 'x'. This reflects the cognitive strategy of retaining essential information while discarding unnecessary details to minimize computational load.
   
   - **Neurobiological Underpinnings:** The authors suggest Hebbian learning and synaptic pruning as potential neurobiological mechanisms underpinning this compression process. These are learning rules where the connection strength between neurons is modified based on their simultaneous activity (Hebb rule) or where excess or unnecessary connections are eliminated to optimize efficiency (synaptic pruning).
   
   - **Machine Learning Analogues:** The Heave axis finds analogies in machine learning techniques such as autoencoders and variational inference, which aim to learn efficient representations of data.

2. **Axis II – Constraint Origin (Sway):**

   - Sway refers to the origin or source of constraints that shape cognitive processes. It's represented by the equation C(z) = Σ_i w_i * g_i(z), where 'w_i' are constraint weights and 'g_i' are constraint functions. This encapsulates how various internal (e.g., neurodevelopmental constraints like pruning, myelination) and external factors (ecological pressures, task demands) influence cognitive representations.
   
   - **Internal vs External Constraints:** The Sway axis distinguishes between inherent developmental constraints (like the energetic costs of brain growth or the information processing limits of neural networks) and environmental/task-related constraints (like problem structure or information demands).

   - **Empirical Evidence from Cross-Domain Studies:** The authors highlight the importance of empirical evidence across diverse cognitive domains to validate this theoretical construct.

3. **Axis III – Temporal Binding (Surge):**

   - Surge pertains to temporal binding or how information is dynamically integrated over time, crucial for cognitive processes like perception and memory. It's represented by the equation δz/δt = f(z;θ) + ∑_i w_i * g_i(z), where 't' represents time, reflecting how the compressed representation 'z' evolves over time under the influence of internal constraints and environmental inputs.
   
   - **Dynamic Systems and Information Flow:** Surge encapsulates dynamic systems theory, illustrating how cognitive processes unfold over time as a result of continuous information flow and self-organizing interactions within the brain.
   
   - **Neural Oscillations and Cognitive Rhythms:** Specific neural oscillations (alpha, theta, gamma) in cortical networks are highlighted as potential mechanisms underlying Surge, suggesting that these rhythmic patterns might synchronize or desynchronize to bind information across time windows relevant for cognition.

   - **Machine Learning Implications: Temporal Credit Assignment:** For machine learning applications, the authors suggest that understanding and modeling temporal dynamics (Surge) can improve algorithms' ability to assign credit for outcomes correctly over time, a challenge known as the "credit assignment problem."

The paper concludes by proposing this Heave-Sway-Surge model as a unifying framework for constraint-limited cognition. It aims to integrate existing cognitive theories (like Global Workspace Theory or Active Inference) under a comprehensive, dynamic systems perspective that accounts for cognitive efficiency, adaptability, and temporal dynamics.
