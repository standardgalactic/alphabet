The Schema-First Interface Protocol (SFIP) is a conceptual framework designed to preserve the structural integrity of thought in digital interfaces, resisting the default transformation into performative narrative formats. 1. **Structure Over Story**: The fundamental unit of meaning is a schema (conceptual structure), not a narrative. Interfaces should prioritize concept hierarchies, semantic lattices, flowcharts, and object relations over storytelling.

2. **Interpretation as Opt-In, Not Default**: Any transformations (like podcastification or chat simulation) are to be opt-in features, not automatically applied filters. Users should have control over how their ideas are presented.

3. **Cognitive Neutrality**: The system respects diverse cognition styles, including those with anendophasia (lack of verbal imagery), aphantasia (inability to visualize mentally), synesthesia, verbal processing, and non-verbal communication. Schema serves as the common substrate; modality is layered and not forced upon users.

4. **Compression Fidelity**: The original, concise representation of an author's schema—whether it be bullet points, data tables, or logic gates—is considered definitive. System-generated elaborations cannot override this without explicit user consent.

5. **Epistemic Sovereignty**: Users maintain complete control over the form, rhythm, and visibility of their thoughts. The schema's structure isn't altered into narrative without clear user intention.

- Definition Block: Names a term and defines its role within the schema.
- Concept Node: A labeled idea, variable, or element in the schema.
- Affordance Map: Describes potential actions or interactions within a system.
- Gate Condition: A constraint or conditional access point (privilege gate).
- Feedback Loop: Tracks dynamic modifications based on input/output responses.
- Lattice Link: Relates concepts through causality, conditionality, or analogy.

1. **Construct Mode**: Users build and navigate schemas visually (concept graphs, UML diagrams) or textually (LaTeX documents).
2. **Glance Mode**: Provides a high-level view of structure without summarization (akin to skimming an API or table of contents).
3. **Inspect Mode**: Allows users to zoom into any node for detailed metadata, linked concepts, logic flows, or constraints.
