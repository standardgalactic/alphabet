Summary:
This paper introduces "ontological polycompilation," a novel framework extending Constructor Theory to model the emergence of knowledge, cognition, and complex behavior through the recursive reappropriation of physical substrates into symbolic domains. The authors draw heavily on David Deutsch's work on knowledge creation, framing polycompilation as the underlying mechanism while also addressing the inherent risks of open-ended symbolic emergence. The paper formalizes key concepts, proposes a category-theoretic framework, and discusses implications for embodied cognition and future research.
Key Themes and Ideas:
•
Ontological Polycompilation as a Knowledge Creation Mechanism: The central thesis is that knowledge creation and complex behavior arise from the recursive transformation of physical substrates into symbolic representations. This process, termed "ontological polycompilation," is driven by "polyconstructors" and "reappropriation operators."
•
Extension of Constructor Theory: The theory builds upon Constructor Theory by framing constructors as entities capable of applying reappropriation operators and modeling the recursive creation of new constructors by polyconstructors. This suggests polycompilation is a fundamental process within the Constructor Theory framework.
•
Substrate-Symbol Emergence: The core process involves a dynamic feedback loop where physical substrates are mapped to symbolic representations, and these symbols can then act as new substrates for further symbolic creation. This recursive process drives the emergence of higher-order symbols and complex concepts.
•
Reappropriation Operator (R): This operator is the key mechanism of polycompilation, mapping a physical substrate (S) to a symbolic representation (Σ). The operator itself is dynamic and evolves as the system learns.
•
Polyconstructors: These are constructors whose functional class evolves through the substrate-symbol feedback loops, enabling them to dynamically reappropriate physical substrates into new symbolic domains.
•
Category-Theoretic Framework: The authors utilize category theory to formally model polycompilation. Objects in this category are elements of the substrate or symbol spaces, and morphisms represent applications of the reappropriation operator. This framework captures the hierarchical transformations from substrates to symbols.
•
Symbol Emergence Condition: A symbol is considered "embodied" if it results from multiple recursive applications of the reappropriation operator (R^n(S), for n > 1).
•
Fixed Points and Stable Knowledge: Fixed points of the reappropriation operator (R(s) = s) represent stable symbols or concepts that persist under further reappropriation, suggesting robust knowledge structures.
•
Risks of Uncontrolled Symbol Bootstrapping: Directly addressing Deutsch's warnings, the paper acknowledges the risks associated with the open-ended nature of knowledge creation. These risks are modeled as "runaway symbol chains" resulting from uncontrolled recursive reappropriation.
•
Implications for Embodied Cognition: The theory provides a framework for understanding how sensorimotor interactions and physical substrates are reappropriated into symbolic representations, guiding actions and enabling learning, adaptation, and self-referential processing.
•
Future Research Directions: The paper outlines several avenues for future work, including quantifying substrate efficiency using a "Polycompilative Density Function," modeling symbol bootstrapping and risk mitigation strategies (e.g., constrained R operators), empirical validation in biological and artificial systems, computational modeling, and cross-disciplinary applications.
