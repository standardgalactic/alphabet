Ontological Polycompilation: Extending Constructor Theory for
Substrate-Symbol Emergence and Knowledge Creation
Anonymous
May 2025
Abstract
Ontological polycompilation extends Constructor Theory [1] to model the recursive reappropriation of phys-
ical substrates into symbolic domains, driving knowledge creation and complex behavior. Inspired by David
Deutschs lecture Knowledge Creation and Its Risks [2], we introduce polyconstructorsdynamic constructors that
evolve through substrate-symbol feedback loopsand formalize their behavior using category theory. We deﬁne
reappropriation operators, the Polycompilation Axiom, and the Symbol Emergence Condition, identifying risks
from runaway symbol chains, such as cognitive ossiﬁcation or unbounded AI inference. A tool-use case study
illustrates the framework, and implications for embodied cognition are explored. We propose risk mitigation
strategies and future directions, including polycompilative density functions, empirical validation, and cross-
disciplinary applications in AI, neuroscience, and linguistics.

Introduction
Constructor Theory [1] frames physical transformations in terms of constructorsentities that perform tasks with-
out degradation. In his lecture Knowledge Creation and Its Risks [2], Deutsch argues that knowledge creation is
humanitys deﬁning capability, but its open-ended nature risks unintended consequences, such as runaway com-
plexity or misaligned systems. We propose ontological polycompilation, a framework where constructors evolve
into polyconstructors that recursively co-opt physical substrates into symbolic domains. This model explains
the emergence of knowledge, cognition, and complex behavior, while addressing risks of uncontrolled symbolic
recursion.

Formal Framework
2.1
Deﬁnitions
Deﬁnition 1 (Substrate and Symbol Spaces). Let S denote the set of physical substrates (e.g., neural tissues,
mechanical components). Let Σ denote the set of symbolic representations associated with elements of S.
Deﬁnition 2 (Constructor). A constructor C transforms an input substrate S ∈S into output S′ ∈S, without
degradation: C : S →S′.
Deﬁnition 3 (Polyconstructor). A polyconstructor Cp is a constructor whose functional class evolves via substrate-
symbol feedback loops, enabling dynamic reappropriation into new symbolic domains.
Deﬁnition 4 (Reappropriation Operator). The reappropriation operator R : S →Σ maps a physical substrate S
to a symbolic representation σ. R is dynamic, adapting as the system learns.
Deﬁnition 5 (Knowledge as Polycompilation). Per [2], knowledge creation is the generation of explanatory
frameworks via polyconstructors, reappropriating substrates into symbolic representations (e.g., concepts, lan-
guages).
2.2
Polycompilation Process
Polycompilation is the recursive application of R:
R(n)(s) = R(R(n−1)(s)),
with R(0)(s) = s
This generates higher-order symbols, enabling complex behaviors and knowledge emergence.

2.3
Category-Theoretic Perspective
We model polycompilation using a category C:
• Objects: Elements of S ∪Σ.
• Morphisms: Applications of R.
Recursive reappropriation is captured by morphism composition:
R(n) = R ◦R ◦· · · ◦R
(n times)
To capture structural emergence, we hypothesize R as a functor R : C →C, preserving composition and enabling
layered transformations. Symbol chains may be modeled as recursive functor applications, with potential monadic
structures for self-referential processing.
2.4
Fixed Points and Symbol Emergence
A ﬁxed point of R satisﬁes:
R(s) = s
Fixed points represent stable symbols or concepts, indicating robust knowledge structures. Embodied symbols
require:
σ = Rn(S),
for n > 1
2.5
Polycompilation Axiom
Axiom 1 (Polycompilation). For any constructor C with access to a reappropriation operator R acting on sub-
strate S, there exists a polyconstructor C′ ∈C(R(S)) such that C′ ⊋C.

Experimental Case Study: Tool Use as Polycompilation
Consider a human hand and stone tool:
• Substrate S1: Hand (grasping substrate).
• Reappropriation R1: Grasping →cutting motion.
• Substrate S2: Hand + stone →gesture space.
• Reappropriation R2: Gesture →communicative icon →cortical remapping.
This yields a knowledge-creating chain:
[grasp] →[tool use] →[gesture] →[language precursor]
Per [2], this exempliﬁes knowledge creation, but risks arise if symbols ossify (e.g., dogmatic rituals).

Implications for Embodied Cognition
Polycompilation explains key aspects of embodied cognition:
• Sensorimotor Integration: Substrates (e.g., sensorimotor interactions) are reappropriated into symbols,
guiding actions.
• Learning and Adaptation: Recursive R builds abstract concepts from prior knowledge.
• Self-Referential Processing: Systems reappropriate their own symbols, enabling meta-cognition.

Connection to Constructor Theory
Constructor Theory [1] focuses on possible and impossible tasks. Polycompilation extends this by:
• Framing constructors as entities applying R.
• Modeling recursive constructor creation, where polyconstructors build new constructors, driving knowledge
emergence.
This aligns with Deutschs view of knowledge as explanatory growth [2].

Risk Analysis and Mitigation
Recursive symbol chains risk:
• Cognitive Ossiﬁcation: Symbols becoming rigid (e.g., dogma).
• Unbounded Inference: AI generating uninterpretable outputs.
• Sociotechnical Debt: Cultural or technological systems becoming unmanageably complex.
Mitigation strategies include:
• Bounded R Operators: Restrict recursion depth or symbol complexity.
• Self-Corrective Mechanisms: Introduce feedback to detect and prune unstable chains.
• PCD Monitoring: Track polycompilative density to identify high-risk systems.

Polycompilative Density Function
We deﬁne:
PCD(x) = # of unique symbolic reappropriations
mass or energy of substrate x
High PCD may correlate with general intelligence or consciousness, but also with instability.

Objections and Responses
8.1
Substrate Neutrality Objection
Claim: Computation is substrate-independent; why focus on reappropriation?
Response: Polycompilation explains the emergence of interpretive layers, not their operation, aligning with
Deutschs substrate-dependent view of knowledge [2].
8.2
Risk Neglect Objection
Claim: Polycompilation ignores risks of recursive symbol emergence.
Response: We explicitly model risks as uncontrolled Rn(S) chains and propose mitigation strategies.

Conclusion
Ontological polycompilation extends Constructor Theory to model knowledge creation and complex behavior
as recursive substrate-symbol reappropriation. Inspired by [2], we formalize polyconstructors, reappropriation
operators, and a category-theoretic framework, addressing risks of runaway symbol chains. This theory bridges
cognitive science, AI, and evolutionary biology, with future work focusing on empirical validation, risk mitigation,
and cross-disciplinary applications.
