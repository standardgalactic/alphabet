Ontological Polycompilation: A Constructor Theory Extension
for Substrate-Symbol Emergence and Knowledge Creation
Anonymous
May 2025
Abstract
We extend Constructor Theory [1] to model ontological polycompilation, a process where constructors re-
cursively reappropriate physical substrates into symbolic domains, driving knowledge creation and complex be-
havior. Drawing on Deutschs Knowledge Creation and Its Risks [2], we frame polycompilation as a mechanism
for emergent symbolic systems, with risks from uncontrolled symbol bootstrapping. We formalize polyconstruc-
tors, reappropriation operators, and a category-theoretic framework, introducing the Polycompilation Axiom and
Symbol Emergence Condition. A tool-use case study illustrates the theory, and objections from classical compu-
tation are addressed. Implications for embodied cognition and future directions, including empirical validation
and computational modeling, are discussed.

Introduction
Constructor Theory [1] describes physical transformations via constructorssystems that perform tasks without
degradation. In his lecture Knowledge Creation and Its Risks [2], David Deutsch argues that knowledge cre-
ation is humanitys deﬁning capability, but its open-ended nature risks unintended consequences, such as runaway
complexity or misaligned systems. We propose ontological polycompilation, where constructors evolve into poly-
constructors that dynamically co-opt physical substrates into symbolic domains through recursive feedback loops.
This framework models the emergence of knowledge, cognition, and complex behavior, while addressing risks
highlighted by Deutsch.

Formal Framework
2.1
Deﬁnitions
Deﬁnition 1 (Substrate and Symbol Spaces). Let S denote the set of physical substrates (e.g., neural tissues,
mechanical components). Let Σ denote the set of symbolic representations associated with elements of S.
Deﬁnition 2 (Constructor). A constructor C transforms an input substrate S ∈S into output S′ ∈S, without
degradation: C : S →S′.
Deﬁnition 3 (Polyconstructor). A polyconstructor Cp is a constructor whose functional class evolves via substrate-
symbol feedback loops, enabling dynamic reappropriation into new symbolic domains.
Deﬁnition 4 (Reappropriation Operator). The reappropriation operator R : S →Σ maps a physical substrate S
to a symbolic representation σ. R evolves dynamically as the system learns.
Deﬁnition 5 (Knowledge as Polycompilation). Per [2], knowledge creation is the generation of explanatory
frameworks via polyconstructors, where substrates are recursively reappropriated into symbolic representations
(e.g., concepts, languages).
2.2
Polycompilation Process
Polycompilation is the recursive application of R:
R(n)(s) = R(R(n−1)(s)),
with R(0)(s) = s
This process generates higher-order symbols, enabling complex behaviors and knowledge emergence.

2.3
Category-Theoretic Perspective
We model polycompilation using a category C:
• Objects: Elements of S ∪Σ.
• Morphisms: Applications of R.
Recursive reappropriation is captured by morphism composition:
R(n) = R ◦R ◦· · · ◦R
(n times)
This framework models hierarchical transformations from substrates to symbols.
2.4
Fixed Points and Symbol Emergence
A ﬁxed point of R satisﬁes:
R(s) = s
Fixed points represent stable symbols or concepts that persist under further reappropriation, indicating robust
knowledge structures.
2.5
Polycompilation Axiom
Axiom 1 (Polycompilation). For any constructor C with access to a reappropriation operator R acting on sub-
strate S, there exists a polyconstructor C′ ∈C(R(S)) such that C′ ⊋C.
2.6
Symbol Emergence Condition
A symbol σ ∈Σ is embodied if:
σ = Rn(S),
for n > 1
Recursive reappropriation drives knowledge creation but risks runaway symbol chains, aligning with Deutschs
warnings [2].

Experimental Case Study: Tool Use as Polycompilation
Consider a human hand and stone tool:
• Substrate S1: Hand (grasping substrate).
• Reappropriation R1: Grasping →cutting motion.
• Substrate S2: Hand + stone →gesture space.
• Reappropriation R2: Gesture →communicative icon →cortical remapping.
This yields a knowledge-creating chain:
[grasp] →[tool use] →[gesture] →[language precursor]
Per [2], this exempliﬁes knowledge creation, but risks arise if symbols ossify (e.g., dogmatic rituals).

Implications for Embodied Cognition
Polycompilation provides a framework for understanding embodied cognition:
• Sensorimotor Integration: Physical substrates (e.g., sensorimotor interactions) are reappropriated into
symbolic representations, guiding actions.
• Learning and Adaptation: Recursive R enables systems to build abstract concepts from prior knowledge.
• Self-Referential Processing: Systems reappropriate their own symbols, enabling meta-cognition and self-
improvement.

5
Connection to Constructor Theory
Constructor Theory [1] focuses on possible and impossible tasks. Polycompilation aligns with this by:
• Framing constructors as entities applying R.
• Modeling recursive constructor creation, where polyconstructors build new constructors, driving complexity
and knowledge emergence.
This suggests polycompilation as a foundational mechanism for knowledge creation within Constructor Theory.

Objections and Responses
6.1
Substrate Neutrality Objection
Claim: Computation is substrate-independent; why focus on physical reappropriation?
Response: Substrate neutrality assumes ﬁxed interpretive layers. Polycompilation explains their emergence,
aligning with Deutschs view of knowledge as substrate-dependent [2].
6.2
Risk Neglect Objection
Claim: Polycompilation ignores risks of recursive symbol emergence.
Response: We model risks (e.g., runaway complexity) as uncontrolled Rn(S) chains, proposing constrained
R operators for mitigation.

• Empirical Validation: Study biological and artiﬁcial systems to identify recursive reappropriation.
• Computational Modeling: Simulate R to study emergent behaviors.
• Cross-Disciplinary Applications: Explore polycompilation in neuroscience, robotics, linguistics, and AI.
8
Conclusion
Ontological polycompilation extends Constructor Theory to model knowledge creation and complex behavior
as recursive substrate-symbol reappropriation. Inspired by [2], we formalize polyconstructors, reappropriation
operators, and a category-theoretic framework, addressing risks of uncontrolled symbol emergence. This theory
bridges cognitive science, AI, and evolutionary biology, with future work focusing on empirical validation and
risk mitigation.
