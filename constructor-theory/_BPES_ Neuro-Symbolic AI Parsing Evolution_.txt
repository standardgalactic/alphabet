The Brashian Parser Evolution System (BPES): A Neuro-Symbolic Framework for Adaptive Parsing and Ontological Self-Revision

Abstract

The Brashian Parser Evolution System (BPES) is a cognitively inspired, evolvable parsing architecture that unifies symbolic composition, vector-grounded semantics, and evolutionary computation to enable adaptive syntactic-semantic interpretation across heterogeneous domains. By leveraging an agent-centric theory of cognition derived from Douglas Brash's stipulative semantics, BPES constructs referentially anchored symbolic frames, iteratively refines parser grammars via genetic algorithms, and recursively evolves its own ontological substrate through meta-genomic feedback. This architecture is especially suited for tasks requiring high semantic fidelity and dynamic conceptual adaptation, such as mathematical reasoning, legal clause parsing, and scientific document structuring.

1. Theoretical Foundations

At its core, BPES operationalizes Brash's thesis that cognition arises from the stipulation of referential structures—i.e., the act of defining what constitutes a "thing" in a given perceptual or conceptual field. Parsing, within this framework, is not merely a syntactic operation but an act of ontological commitment: the assignment of symbolic structure to an otherwise amorphous stream of linguistic material. Accordingly, BPES treats parsing as an evolvable mapping from linear token sequences to compositional, grounded, and observer-relative representations.

Each parser is encoded as a genome comprising mutable gene sequences:

Attachment Heuristics: Define probabilistic policies for phrase attachment (e.g., PP left-attachment vs right-attachment).

Meta-Genes: Govern higher-order behavior such as symbol creation, ontology merging, and domain adaptation.

3. Symbol Registry and Meta-Genome Integration

BPES includes a dynamic Symbol Registry, which maps symbolic tokens to multimodal embeddings, type signatures, and contextual usage patterns. A meta-genomic layer governs this registry's evolution, enabling:

Symbol induction from high-frequency unknowns

Ontological fusion across domains via embedding proximity and co-occurrence statistics

Registry pruning via usage decay functions and informativeness metrics


The registry thus serves as both a semantic anchor and an evolving ontological scaffold.

4. System Dynamics and Lifecycle

4.1 Evolutionary Parsing Loop

1. Initialization: A population of parser genomes is instantiated or retrieved from snapshot storage.


2. Corpus Evaluation: Each genome parses a corpus segment and is evaluated on grounding coherence, structural interpretability, ambiguity collapse, and downstream task utility (e.g., clause labeling, theorem resolution).


3. Selection and Reproduction: Top-performing genomes undergo elite selection, crossover, and mutation.


4. Meta-genomic Adjustments: Frequent grounding failures trigger symbol creation and registry updates.


5. Deployment: High-fitness genomes are deployed to production pods; low-fitness genomes are retired or recycled.



4.2 LLM Feedback Loop

BPES can operate as a symbolic-scaffolding agent for LLMs:

Parse-Guided Attention: Attention masks are modulated to reflect syntactic constraints inferred by BPES.

Semantic Verification: Generated texts are parsed and validated post hoc for referential coherence.

Repair Prompts: Parsing failures generate feedback prompts for autoregressive correction.
