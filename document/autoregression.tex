\begin{abstract}
This paper develops a comprehensive mathematical framework unifying three previously separate research programs: (i) discrete autoregressive systems such as large language models and cellular automata; (ii) continuous field-theoretic dynamics modeled through the Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework; and (iii) a novel \emph{constraint-first} ontology grounded in admissibility manifolds, distinction geometry, and repair pseudometrics. By employing derived algebraic geometry, symplectic reduction, and information-theoretic entropic smoothing, we demonstrate that discrete cognitive and computational processes are reflective subcategories of continuous field-theoretic models—and that both regimes admit a deeper characterization in terms of the \emph{cost of making and maintaining distinctions}. 

Central to the framework is the entropic smoothing comonad \(\Stau\), which compresses structured field data while preserving semantic topology, mirroring processes of abstraction and generalization in both neural and artificial cognitive systems. We introduce the \emph{admissibility manifold} \(\Adm(X)\) as the locus of field configurations consistent with a given epistemic context, and define the \emph{repair pseudometric} \(\dRP\) quantifying the minimal deformation cost required to restore an inadmissible state to admissibility. The resulting \emph{geometry of distinctions} provides a unified account of observability, erasure, witness propagation, and the conservation of irreducible ambiguity.

Extensive proofs establish embeddings, adjunctions, and symplectic reductions; numerical simulations validate trajectory alignment between discrete and continuous models; and diagrammatic illustrations clarify categorical structure throughout. The framework integrates simulated agency, semantic infrastructure, trajectory-aware recursive tiling (TARTAN), chain of memory (CoM), distinction ecology, and diffusion-based physical simulation (PHYSIFORMER), alongside cosmological parallels and implications for AI alignment. Future directions include quantum extensions, multi-modal integration, meta-cognitive modeling, and experimental predictions for distinction-cost observables in biological neural circuits.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Motivations and Central Questions}

The question of how structure persists through change—how a mind retains its identity across time, how a field evolves while conserving invariants, how a language model maintains semantic coherence across thousands of tokens—is among the deepest and most cross-disciplinary problems in science. Aristotle posed an early version of it in terms of form and matter; Newton encoded it in the conservation of momentum; Turing approached it through the invariance of computability under encoding \cite{Turing1950, Newton1687, Lagrange1788}. The present work proposes that a single mathematical framework, built from derived geometry, entropic smoothing, and distinction theory, can illuminate persistence across all of these scales simultaneously.

The framework unifies three distinct but mutually illuminating research programs. The first is the study of \emph{discrete autoregressive systems}: large language models (LLMs), cellular automata (CAs), and related architectures in which a state at time $t+1$ is determined by a rule applied to the state at time $t$ \cite{Vaswani2017, Brown2020, Wolfram2002}. The second is the RSVP (Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum) field-theoretic framework, in which scalar density $\Phi$, vector flow $\bv$, and entropy density $S$ evolve together on a manifold $X$ under Hamiltonian dynamics that preserve a derived symplectic structure \cite{Arnold1989, Marsden1974, Carroll2019}. The third is a \emph{constraint-first ontology} built around admissibility manifolds, repair pseudometrics, and the geometry of distinctions—a program holding that the fundamental object of study is not the state of a system but rather the constraints that determine which states are reachable, observable, and semantically coherent \cite{Shannon1948, Jaynes1957, Cover1991}.

These three programs are not merely analogous; they are mathematically interlocking. The core result of this paper is that discrete autoregressive dynamics embed faithfully into the RSVP field-theoretic framework via an entropic smoothing adjunction, and that both the discrete and continuous regimes are best understood through the lens of distinction geometry: the algebra of what can be told apart, at what cost, and with what degree of irreducible residual ambiguity.

\section{Foundations of Autoregressive Systems}
\label{sec:autoregressive}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Historical Background}

Autoregressive modeling has roots extending from Yule's 1927 work on sunspot time-series \cite{Yule1927} through McCulloch and Pitts' formal neurons \cite{Rosenblatt1958}, Elman's recurrent networks \cite{Rumelhart1986}, and the modern transformer architecture of Vaswani et al.\ \cite{Vaswani2017}. The unifying intuition is that the next state of a system can be well-predicted from a sufficient window of its past states—that the future is, in some sense, already latent in the structured present.

Von Neumann's cellular automata \cite{Neumann1966} showed that this principle admits a purely spatial instantiation: a grid of cells updating in parallel according to local rules can give rise to unbounded computational universality and self-reproducing structures. Conway's Game of Life \cite{Conway1970, Gardner1970} made this vivid: from two rules about neighbor counts, a rich ecology of persistent patterns—gliders, oscillators, eaters—emerges spontaneously. The lesson, which the present framework formalizes, is that \emph{global cognitive or computational structure can emerge from local constraint satisfaction}.

\subsection{Formal Definitions}

\begin{definition}[Autoregressive System]
\label{def:ar_system}
An \emph{autoregressive system} is a triple $(S, U, k)$ where $S$ is a finite set of states, $k \geq 1$ is the context window, and $U: S^k \to S$ is the update rule. The induced dynamics on the sequence space $S^{\N}$ are given by $(s_t)_{t \geq 0} \mapsto (s_{t+1})_{t \geq 0}$ where $s_{t+1} = U(s_{t-k+1}, \ldots, s_t)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The Bicategory $\ARfin$]
\label{def:arfin}
The bicategory $\ARfin$ of finite autoregressive systems is defined as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Objects}: Finite sets $S$ (state spaces), with cardinality $|S| < \infty$.
    \item \textbf{1-Morphisms}: Update functors $U: S^k \to S$, together with morphisms of context windows.
    \item \textbf{2-Morphisms}: Natural transformations $\alpha: U \Rightarrow V$ representing rule deformations.
    \item \textbf{Monoidal structure}: $S \otimes T = S \times T$ (parallel composition of systems).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The monoidal structure on $\ARfin$ encodes synchronous parallel execution of independent autoregressive systems. This will later correspond, under the embedding $\iotaf$, to the tensor product of field configurations in $\dSt$.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Large Language Models]
\label{ex:llm}
An LLM with hidden state $h_t \in \R^d$ (typically $d \in \{512, 1024, 4096\}$) evolves via:
\[
h_{t+1} = f_\theta(h_t, x_t),
\]
where $x_t$ is the input token embedding and $f_\theta$ is a composition of multi-head attention layers and feed-forward networks. Under mild discretization, $h_t$ takes values in a finite approximation $S \subset \R^d$, making this a valid object of $\ARfin$ \cite{Vaswani2017, Brown2020}.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Cellular Automaton as Autoregressive System]
\label{ex:ca}
Let $G = \Z^2$ be the integer lattice. A CA state is $s \in \{0,1\}^G$, and the update rule $U$ maps each cell $g \in G$ to a function of its Moore neighborhood $\mathcal{N}(g) = \{g + \delta : \delta \in \{-1,0,1\}^2\}$. Under periodic boundary conditions on a finite subgraph, this is an object of $\ARfin$ with $|S| = 2^{|\text{grid}|}$ \cite{Wolfram2002, Conway1970}.
\end{example}

\subsection{Information-Theoretic Properties}

The entropy of an autoregressive system at time $t$ is:
\[
H_t = -\sum_{s \in S} p_t(s) \log p_t(s),
\]
where $p_t$ is the marginal distribution over states at time $t$. Under a deterministic update $U$, entropy is non-increasing: $H_{t+1} \leq H_t$, with equality when $U$ is a bijection. The \emph{entropy loss} $\Delta H_t = H_t - H_{t+1} \geq 0$ quantifies information destroyed by each update step.

\begin{proposition}[Entropy Monotonicity]
\label{prop:entropy_mono}
For any autoregressive system $(S, U, k)$ with a deterministic update rule $U$ and an initial distribution $p_0$ over $S^k$, the sequence $(H_t)_{t \geq 0}$ is non-increasing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The update map $U: S^k \to S$ induces a pushforward $U_* p_t = p_{t+1}$. Since $U$ is a deterministic (hence measure-preserving-up-to-collapse) map, the data-processing inequality gives $H(U_* p) \leq H(p)$ \cite{Cover1991}. More precisely, let $Y = U(X_1, \ldots, X_k)$. By the chain rule of entropy: $H(X_1, \ldots, X_k) = H(Y) + H(X_1, \ldots, X_k | Y) \geq H(Y)$, and the result follows since $H_{t+1} = H(Y)$ and $H_t \leq H(X_1, \ldots, X_k)$ by the memoryless approximation.
\end{proof}

\section{Continuous Dynamical Frameworks: Field Evolutions and Symplectic Structures}
\label{sec:continuous}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{The RSVP Framework}

The Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework models the evolution of three coupled fields on a compact manifold $X$ (typically $X = S^3$, $X = T^3$, or a more general Riemannian manifold):
\begin{align}
\Phi &: X \times \R_{\geq 0} \to \R & &\text{(scalar density, encoding semantic mass or information concentration),} \\
\bv &: X \times \R_{\geq 0} \to TX & &\text{(vector flow field, encoding directed information transport),} \\
S &: X \times \R_{\geq 0} \to \R_{\geq 0} & &\text{(entropy density, encoding local uncertainty or disorder).}
\end{align}

These fields are coupled through a system of PDEs derived from a variational principle. The RSVP action functional is:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}[\Phi, \bv, S] = \int_X \int_0^T \left[ \frac{1}{2} |\partial_t \Phi|^2 - \frac{1}{2}|\nabla \Phi|^2 - V(\Phi) + \bv \cdot \nabla S - \frac{\tau}{2} S \log S \right] \mathrm{dVol}_X \, dt,
\end{equation}
where $V(\Phi)$ is a self-interaction potential (e.g., $V(\Phi) = \lambda \Phi^4 / 4$) and $\tau > 0$ is the entropic coupling constant. The Euler-Lagrange equations give:
\begin{align}
\partial_t^2 \Phi &= \Delta \Phi - V'(\Phi) + \text{back-reaction from } S, \label{eq:phi_eom} \\
\partial_t \bv &= -(\bv \cdot \nabla)\bv - \nabla p + \nu \Delta \bv, \label{eq:v_eom} \\
\partial_t S &= -\nabla \cdot (S\bv) + \tau \Delta S. \label{eq:s_eom}
\end{align}

Equation~\eqref{eq:s_eom} is a \emph{Fokker-Planck equation} for the entropy density, coupling advection by the flow $\bv$ with diffusion controlled by $\tau$. This structure is central to the connection with diffusion-based generative models (see Section~\ref{sec:physiformer}).

\subsection{Derived Symplectic Structure}

The phase space of the RSVP system is most naturally formulated as a derived stack. Let $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{F}_\Phi \times \mathcal{F}_{\bv} \times \mathcal{F}_S$ be the field sheaf, and define:
\[
\X = \mathbf{R}\mathrm{Map}(X, \mathcal{F}),
\]
the derived mapping stack encoding all field configurations together with their derived (homotopical) structure \cite{Lurie2009, Toen2014}. The AKSZ formalism \cite{AKSZ1997} equips $\X$ with a $(-1)$-shifted symplectic form $\omega_{-1} \in \Omega^{2, \mathrm{cl}}(\X)[-1]$, defined by:
\[
\omega_{-1}(\delta_1, \delta_2) = \int_X \langle \delta_1 \Phi \wedge \delta_2 \bv \rangle \, \mathrm{dVol}_X,
\]
where $\delta_i$ are tangent vectors at a field configuration \cite{PTVV2013}.

The Hamiltonian is:
\[
S_{\mathrm{AKSZ}} = \int_X \left[ \frac{1}{2}|\nabla \Phi|^2 + V(\Phi) + \half S \log S - \bv \cdot \nabla S \right] \mathrm{dVol}_X,
\]
and the BV bracket gives the Hamiltonian vector field $Q_H = \BV{S_{\mathrm{AKSZ}}, \cdot}$.

\begin{proposition}[Symplectic Preservation]
\label{prop:symplectic}
The Hamiltonian flow $Q_H$ preserves $\omega_{-1}$: $\mathcal{L}_{Q_H} \omega_{-1} = 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Cartan's magic formula, $\mathcal{L}_{Q_H} \omega_{-1} = d(\iota_{Q_H} \omega_{-1}) + \iota_{Q_H} (d\omega_{-1})$. Since $\omega_{-1}$ is closed, $d\omega_{-1} = 0$. By Hamilton's equation in the BV formalism, $\iota_{Q_H} \omega_{-1} = -\delta S_{\mathrm{AKSZ}}$ (the graded de Rham differential of the action). Therefore:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{Q_H} \omega_{-1} = d(-\delta S_{\mathrm{AKSZ}}) = -\delta^2 S_{\mathrm{AKSZ}} = 0,
\]
since the de Rham differential $d$ and the BV differential $\delta$ anticommute on a $(-1)$-shifted symplectic manifold \cite{Arnold1989, Marsden1974, AKSZ1997, PTVV2013}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Noether Currents and Conservation Laws}

The RSVP system admits a family of Noether currents associated with symmetries of $\mathcal{A}$. Under a volume-preserving diffeomorphism $\phi: X \to X$ (which we interpret as a \emph{semantic reparametrization}), the current:
\[
J^\mu[\phi] = \Phi \, \partial^\mu \phi^\nu \, \partial_\nu \Phi + S \, \phi^\nu \bv_\nu
\]
is conserved: $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$ on-shell. This conservation law will play a role in Section~\ref{sec:conservation}, where we establish that irreducible ambiguity is itself a conserved quantity under admissible field transformations.

\section{Embedding Mechanisms: From Discrete States to Derived Stacks}
\label{sec:embedding}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Motivation and Strategy}

The embedding of discrete autoregressive systems into the continuous RSVP framework proceeds in two steps. First, each finite state set $S \in \ARfin$ is mapped to a discrete derived stack $\underline{S} \in \dSt$—its ``constant stack'' incarnation, in which all field values are step functions. Second, update morphisms $U: S \to S$ in $\ARfin$ are mapped to endomorphisms of $\underline{S}$ in $\dSt$. The key theorem is that this map is faithful, injective on morphisms, and monoidal.

Geometrically, this is analogous to embedding a time-lapse sequence of photographs into a smooth film: the photographs are there as individual frames, but the continuous film contains additional structure (motion blur, interpolation, derivative information) that the discrete sequence cannot represent directly. The embedding does not destroy information; it adds structure.

\begin{proposition}[Faithful Embedding]
\label{prop:embedding}
The functor $\iotaf: \ARfin \to \dSt$ defined by $\iotaf(S) = \underline{S}$ (the constant derived stack on $S$) and $\iotaf(U) = \underline{U}$ is faithful.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We must show that for any two 1-morphisms $U, V: S \to T$ in $\ARfin$, if $\iotaf(U) = \iotaf(V)$ in $\dSt$, then $U = V$. By construction, $\underline{U}$ acts on the underlying sets of points of $\underline{S}$ (its $\pi_0$) exactly as $U$ acts on $S$. The Yoneda lemma for derived stacks gives:
\[
\mathrm{Hom}_{\dSt}(\underline{S}, \underline{T}) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Set}}(S, T) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\ARfin}(S, T),
\]
where the last equality holds because morphisms in $\ARfin$ with trivial context window ($k = 1$) reduce to set maps \cite{Lurie2009, Eilenberg1945}. Faithfulness is an immediate consequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Monoidality]
\label{cor:monoidal}
$\iotaf$ is a strong monoidal functor: $\iotaf(S \otimes T) \cong \underline{S} \times \underline{T}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The cartesian product of constant derived stacks is the constant stack on the cartesian product of underlying sets: $\underline{S} \times \underline{T} \cong \underline{S \times T} = \underline{S \otimes T} = \iotaf(S \otimes T)$ \cite{Toen2014}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Image of the Embedding}

Not every derived stack lies in the image of $\iotaf$. The image consists precisely of those derived stacks $\mathcal{Y} \in \dSt$ satisfying: (i) $\mathcal{Y}$ is $0$-truncated (no nontrivial higher homotopy groups); (ii) the underlying topological space $|\mathcal{Y}|$ is discrete; and (iii) $\mathcal{Y}$ is locally of finite presentation. This characterizes the \emph{discrete locus} $\dSt^{\mathrm{disc}} \subset \dSt$, and $\iotaf$ is an equivalence $\ARfin \xrightarrow{\sim} \dSt^{\mathrm{disc}}$.

The continuous RSVP stacks, by contrast, have nontrivial cotangent complexes $\mathbb{L}_\X \neq 0$ and carry genuine derived structure encoding the deformation theory of field configurations around any given point.

\section{Entropic Smoothing: Mathematical Formulation and Proofs}
\label{sec:entropic}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{The Entropic Smoothing Functional}

Entropic smoothing is a variational procedure that deforms a field configuration $(\Phi, \bv, S)$ toward a smoother representative in its ``semantic neighborhood,'' penalizing both departure from a reference density $\bar{S}_\tau$ and high-frequency oscillations in the fields. The functional is:
\begin{align}
\Etau(\Phi, \bv, S) &= \int_X \Bigg[ S \log \frac{S}{\bar{S}_\tau(\Phi, \bv)} + \frac{\tau}{2} \|\nabla^k \mu(\Phi, \bv, S)\|^2 \nonumber \\
&\quad + \lambda_\Phi \|\nabla \Phi\|^2 + \lambda_{\bv} \|\nabla \bv\|^2 \Bigg] \mathrm{dVol}_X.
\label{eq:entropic_functional}
\end{align}

Here $\bar{S}_\tau(\Phi, \bv)$ is a $\tau$-dependent reference density constructed from $\Phi$ and $\bv$ by a mollification procedure:
\[
\bar{S}_\tau(\Phi, \bv)(x) = \int_X K_\tau(x, y) \, g(\Phi(y), \bv(y)) \, \mathrm{dVol}_X(y),
\]
where $K_\tau(x, y) = (4\pi\tau)^{-n/2} \exp(-d(x,y)^2 / 4\tau)$ is the heat kernel on $X$ (with $n = \dim X$) and $g$ is a smooth positive coupling function. The first term in~\eqref{eq:entropic_functional} is the relative entropy (Kullback-Leibler divergence) of $S$ against $\bar{S}_\tau$; the second penalizes $k$-th order derivatives of the moment $\mu = \int_X x \, S(x) \, dx$ (center of semantic mass); the third and fourth regularize $\Phi$ and $\bv$ respectively.

\begin{lemma}[Existence and Uniqueness of Minimizer]
\label{lem:minimizer}
For any $\tau > 0$ and any $L^2$ field configuration $(\Phi_0, \bv_0, S_0)$ with $S_0 > 0$ a.e.\ and $\int_X S_0 \, \mathrm{dVol}_X = 1$, the functional $\Etau$ admits a unique minimizer $(\Phi^*, \bv^*, S^*)$ in the Sobolev space $H^k(X)^3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\Etau$ is strictly convex in $S$ (since $s \mapsto s \log s$ is strictly convex) and strictly convex in $\Phi, \bv$ (due to the Sobolev gradient penalty). Coercivity follows from the KL divergence term: $\Etau \to +\infty$ whenever $\|S\|_{L^1}$ or $\|\Phi\|_{H^k}$ diverges. By the direct method in the calculus of variations, any minimizing sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence in $H^k$, and the functional is weakly lower semicontinuous (being convex and continuous). Strict convexity yields uniqueness \cite{Evans1998, Villani2008}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Entropic Smoothing Comonad}

The assignment $(\Phi, \bv, S) \mapsto (\Phi^*, \bv^*, S^*)$ defines a natural transformation on $\dSt$, which we package as a comonad.

\begin{definition}[Entropic Smoothing Comonad]
\label{def:comonad}
The \emph{entropic smoothing comonad} $\Stau: \dSt \to \dSt$ is defined by:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On objects: $\Stau(\X) = \Ctau / \mathcal{L}_\tau$, where $\Ctau = \{(\Phi, \bv, S) \in \X : \delta \Etau / \delta S = 0\}$ is the critical locus of $\Etau$ and $\mathcal{L}_\tau$ is the gauge group of $\tau$-scale diffeomorphisms.
    \item On morphisms: $\Stau$ acts by precomposition with the canonical inclusion $\Ctau \hookrightarrow \X$.
    \item \textbf{Counit} $\epsilon: \Stau(\X) \to \X$: the inclusion of the smoothed locus into the full field space.
    \item \textbf{Comultiplication} $\Delta: \Stau \to \Stau \circ \Stau$: applying smoothing at scale $\tau$ to the already-smoothed configuration at scale $\tau$ produces the smoothed configuration at scale $2\tau$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Comonad Laws]
\label{prop:comonad}
$(\Stau, \epsilon, \Delta)$ satisfies the comonad laws: counitality ($(\epsilon \circ \Stau) \circ \Delta = \mathrm{id} = (\Stau \circ \epsilon) \circ \Delta$) and coassociativity ($((\Stau \circ \Delta) \circ \Delta = (\Delta \circ \Stau) \circ \Delta$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Counitality.} By definition, $\Stau(\X)$ consists of the $\tau$-critical configurations. Applying $\epsilon$ recovers the underlying configuration in $\X$, and then $\Stau$ re-smooths it; since the critical locus is already smooth at scale $\tau$, re-smoothing is idempotent: $\Stau(\epsilon(\sigma)) = \sigma$ for $\sigma \in \Stau(\X)$.

\textbf{Coassociativity.} Smoothing at scale $\tau$ twice in sequence (comultiplication followed by smoothing) is equivalent to smoothing once at scale $2\tau$ (by the semigroup property of the heat kernel: $K_\tau * K_\tau = K_{2\tau}$ on $X$). Both compositions $(\Stau \circ \Delta)$ and $(\Delta \circ \Stau)$ produce the $2\tau$-smoothed configuration, establishing coassociativity \cite{MacLane1998}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Semantic Interpretation}

The comonad $\Stau$ admits a semantic interpretation that connects it to neural and cognitive processing. In an LLM, successive transformer layers perform a kind of iterative entropic smoothing: each layer compresses the high-dimensional token representation toward a lower-dimensional semantic attractor, discarding high-frequency syntactic detail while preserving low-frequency semantic structure. The parameter $\tau$ corresponds to the ``semantic bandwidth'' of the model—the scale at which distinctions are considered meaningful rather than noise.

More precisely, the attention mechanism in a transformer layer can be written as:
\[
\mathrm{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V,
\]
which is a weighted averaging operation—precisely the form of entropic smoothing at scale $\tau = 1/\sqrt{d_k}$. The depth of the network corresponds to iterated applications of $\Stau$, aligning exactly with the comonadic structure of Definition~\ref{def:comonad}.

\section{Categorical Adjunctions and Reflective Subcategories}
\label{sec:adjunction}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{The Main Adjunction}

\begin{theorem}[Main Adjunction]
\label{thm:adjunction}
There exists an adjunction:
\[
\Stau \dashv \iotaf: \ARfin \rightleftarrows \RSVPomega,
\]
where $\RSVPomega$ denotes the full subcategory of $\dSt$ consisting of derived stacks equipped with a $(-1)$-shifted symplectic form. Under this adjunction, $\ARfin$ is a reflective subcategory of $\RSVPomega$: the counit $\epsilon: \Stau \circ \iotaf \to \mathrm{id}_{\ARfin}$ is a natural isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We construct the unit and counit explicitly.

\textbf{Unit} $\eta: \mathrm{id}_{\RSVPomega} \to \iotaf \circ \Stau$. For $\X \in \RSVPomega$, $\Stau(\X)$ is a derived stack with discrete $\pi_0$ (since the critical locus of $\Etau$ consists of isolated smooth configurations modulo gauge). The unit $\eta_\X: \X \to \iotaf(\Stau(\X))$ maps each field configuration to its entropic smoothing limit.

\textbf{Counit} $\epsilon: \Stau \circ \iotaf \to \mathrm{id}_{\ARfin}$. For $S \in \ARfin$, $\iotaf(S) = \underline{S}$ is a discrete stack. Entropic smoothing of a discrete stack at scale $\tau$ returns the same discrete stack (there is no ``smoothing'' to be done—$\underline{S}$ already lies in $\Ctau$ since it has no gradient). Thus $\Stau(\iotaf(S)) \cong \underline{S}$, and $\epsilon_S: \Stau(\iotaf(S)) \xrightarrow{\sim} S$ is a natural isomorphism.

\textbf{Triangular identities.} We must verify $(\epsilon_{\Stau X} \circ \Stau \eta_X) = \mathrm{id}_{\Stau X}$ and $(\iotaf \epsilon_S \circ \eta_{\iotaf S}) = \mathrm{id}_{\iotaf S}$. The first follows from the comonad law $(\epsilon \circ \Stau) \circ \Delta = \mathrm{id}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:comonad}). The second follows from the faithfulness of $\iotaf$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:embedding}) \cite{Lurie2009, MacLane1998}.

Reflectivity follows from the fact that $\epsilon$ is a natural isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\section{Symplectic Reductions and Lossy Compressions}

\subsection{The Moment Map and Constraint Locus}

The entropic functional $\Etau$ defines a moment map $\mtau: \X \to \mathfrak{g}^*$, where $\mathfrak{g}$ is the Lie algebra of the group $\mathcal{G}_\tau$ of $\tau$-scale gauge transformations. The constraint locus is:
\[
\Ctau = \mu_\tau^{-1}(0) = \{(\Phi, \bv, S) \in \X : \delta \Etau / \delta S = 0\},
\]
and the reduced phase space is:
\[
\Xred = \Ctau / \mathcal{L}_\tau,
\]
where $\mathcal{L}_\tau \subset \mathcal{G}_\tau$ is the isotropy group. This is the Marsden-Weinstein reduction of the RSVP system at level $0$ \cite{Marsden1974}.

\begin{proposition}[Inherited Symplectic Structure]
\label{prop:reduction}
$\Xred$ inherits a $(-1)$-shifted symplectic form $\omega_{\mathrm{red}} = \iota^* \omega_{-1} / \mathcal{L}_\tau$, where $\iota: \Ctau \hookrightarrow \X$ is the inclusion.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $\Ctau$ is coisotropic with respect to $\omega_{-1}$: $\ker(\omega_{-1}|_{\Ctau}) = T\mathcal{L}_\tau$. The reduction $\Xred = \Ctau / \mathcal{L}_\tau$ is therefore equipped with the unique symplectic form $\omega_{\mathrm{red}}$ satisfying $\pi^* \omega_{\mathrm{red}} = \iota^* \omega_{-1}$, where $\pi: \Ctau \to \Xred$ is the quotient map. Coisotropicity follows from the moment map condition $\iota_{\xi_\X} \omega_{-1} = \delta \langle \mtau, \xi \rangle$ for $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$ \cite{PTVV2013, Marsden1974, AKSZ1997}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lossy Compression and Semantic Coarse-Graining}

The reduction $\X \twoheadrightarrow \Xred$ is a \emph{lossy compression}: information in the ``gauge directions'' $T\mathcal{L}_\tau$ is irretrievably discarded. The information lost is precisely the sub-$\tau$ scale fluctuations of the fields—the semantic ``noise'' that the comonad $\Stau$ is designed to filter.

\section{Admissibility Manifolds and the Geometry of Constraints}
\label{sec:admissibility}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{The Admissibility Manifold}

A central claim of the constraint-first ontology is that the fundamental object of study in any cognitive or physical system is not ``what states the system is in'' but rather ``what states the system could coherently be in, given its context.'' This is formalized by the admissibility manifold.

\begin{definition}[Admissibility Manifold]
\label{def:admissibility}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an \emph{epistemic context}—a structured collection of observational constraints, background theories, and semantic commitments. The \emph{admissibility manifold} $\Adm(\mathcal{C}) \subset \X$ is the submanifold of field configurations consistent with $\mathcal{C}$:
\[
\Adm(\mathcal{C}) = \{(\Phi, \bv, S) \in \X : \forall c \in \mathcal{C},\ c(\Phi, \bv, S) = 0\},
\]
where each $c \in \mathcal{C}$ is a smooth constraint function $c: \X \to \R$.
\end{definition}

The admissibility manifold generalizes several classical constructions. When $\mathcal{C}$ consists of energy constraints, $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ is the energy surface of Hamiltonian mechanics. When $\mathcal{C}$ consists of consistency constraints for a logical theory, $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ is the model space of that theory. When $\mathcal{C}$ encodes the training data of an LLM, $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ is (approximately) the set of text distributions consistent with the corpus.

\begin{proposition}[Admissibility is Closed Under RSVP Flow]
\label{prop:adm_closed}
If the constraints $\mathcal{C}$ are preserved by the RSVP Hamiltonian flow $Q_H$ (i.e., $\{c, S_{\mathrm{AKSZ}}\}_{\mathrm{BV}} = 0$ for all $c \in \mathcal{C}$), then $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ is invariant under $Q_H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $(\Phi, \bv, S) \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$ and any $c \in \mathcal{C}$:
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0} c(e^{tQ_H}(\Phi, \bv, S)) = Q_H(c) = \{c, S_{\mathrm{AKSZ}}\}_{\mathrm{BV}} = 0,
\]
by hypothesis. Thus $e^{tQ_H}(\Phi, \bv, S) \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$ for all $t$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Admissibility and the Entropic Smoothing Comonad}

A key structural result connects admissibility to entropic smoothing.

\begin{theorem}[Smoothing Stays Admissible]
\label{thm:smooth_adm}
For any linear constraint $c \in \mathcal{C}$ (i.e., $c(\Phi, \bv, S) = \int_X f \cdot \Phi \, d\mathrm{Vol}$ for $f \in C^\infty(X)$), the entropic smoothing operator $\Stau$ maps $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ to itself: $\Stau(\Adm(\mathcal{C})) \subseteq \Adm(\mathcal{C})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $(\Phi^*, \bv^*, S^*)$ be the minimizer of $\Etau$ subject to $(\Phi, \bv, S) \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$. The minimization is a constrained optimization problem; by the KKT conditions, the Lagrange multipliers enforce $c(\Phi^*, \bv^*, S^*) = 0$ at the minimizer. For linear constraints, the Lagrange multiplier condition is exactly that $c$ is satisfied, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\section{The Repair Pseudometric and Distinction Geometry}
\label{sec:repair}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Inadmissibility and Repair}

In practice, neither natural nor artificial cognitive systems remain perfectly within their admissibility manifold. Perturbations—noise, adversarial inputs, drift in distributional context—can push a state $\sigma \in \X$ off $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$. The central question then becomes: how far off is $\sigma$, and at what cost can it be restored?

\begin{definition}[Repair Pseudometric]
\label{def:repair}
For a state $\sigma \in \X$ and a context $\mathcal{C}$, the \emph{repair cost} is:
\[
\dRP(\sigma, \Adm(\mathcal{C})) = \inf \left\{ \int_0^1 \sqrt{\Etau(\dot\gamma(t))} \, dt : \gamma(0) = \sigma,\ \gamma(1) \in \Adm(\mathcal{C}) \right\},
\]
where the infimum is over all smooth paths $\gamma: [0,1] \to \X$ with the indicated boundary conditions. The \emph{repair pseudometric} on $\X$ is $\dRP(\sigma, \sigma') = \dRP(\sigma, \Adm(\mathcal{C})) + \dRP(\sigma', \Adm(\mathcal{C})) - 2\inf_{\rho \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})} (\dRP(\sigma, \rho) + \dRP(\sigma', \rho))$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The repair pseudometric is a genuine pseudometric (not a metric) because states at different points of $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ may have zero repair distance from each other, even when they are not identical as field configurations. This reflects the semantic equivalence of configurations related by $\tau$-scale gauge transformations.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Repair as Optimal Transport]
\label{prop:repair_ot}
The repair cost $\dRP(\sigma, \Adm(\mathcal{C}))$ is equal to the $W_2$ Wasserstein distance (with ground metric given by $\Etau$) between the entropy density $S$ of $\sigma$ and the nearest admissible entropy density $S^* \in \{S' : (\Phi', \bv', S') \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})\}$:
\[
\dRP(\sigma, \Adm(\mathcal{C})) = \Wtwo(S, S^*).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the Benamou-Brenier formula, the $W_2$ distance between two probability densities equals the infimum of the kinetic energy of paths in density space connecting them \cite{Villani2008}. The kinetic energy with respect to the $\Etau$ metric is exactly $\int_0^1 \Etau(\dot\gamma) \, dt$, giving the result.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Distinction Geometry}

The repair pseudometric induces a geometry on the space of distinctions—pairs of states that a cognitive system can tell apart.

\begin{definition}[Distinction Space]
\label{def:distinction}
The \emph{distinction space} $\Dist(\X, \mathcal{C})$ is the metric space $(\X, \dRP)$ modulo the equivalence relation $\sigma \sim \sigma'$ when $\dRP(\sigma, \sigma') = 0$. Two states are \emph{distinguishable} if $[\sigma] \neq [\sigma']$ in $\Dist(\X, \mathcal{C})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Finiteness of Distinction Space]
\label{prop:fin_dist}
For $\tau > 0$ and compact $X$, $\Dist(\X, \mathcal{C})$ has finite Minkowski dimension:
\[
\dim_M \Dist(\X, \mathcal{C}) \leq n / \tau,
\]
where $n = \dim X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The metric $\dRP$ at scale $\tau$ cannot distinguish features smaller than $\sqrt{\tau}$ (by the heat kernel bound $K_\tau(x,y) \leq C$ for $d(x,y) \leq \sqrt{\tau}$). Thus the covering number $N(\Dist(\X, \mathcal{C}), \epsilon)$ at scale $\epsilon$ is bounded by the number of $\epsilon$-balls of radius $\sqrt{\tau}$ needed to cover $X$, which scales as $(\mathrm{Vol}(X) / \epsilon^n) \cdot \tau^{-n/2}$. The Minkowski dimension estimate follows by standard covering number arguments \cite{Cover1991}.
\end{proof}

This finiteness result has an important cognitive science interpretation: a cognitive system operating at semantic scale $\tau$ can maintain at most $O(\tau^{-n/2})$ distinct conceptual distinctions simultaneously. Increasing semantic resolution (decreasing $\tau$) costs computational resources exponentially.

\section{Observability, Witnesses, and the Geometry of Evidence}
\label{sec:witnesses}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Observability as Restorability}

A key conceptual move in the constraint-first program is to define \emph{observability} not as direct access to a state, but as the ability to \emph{restore} a state from evidence. A state $\sigma$ is observable from evidence $e$ if there exists a repair path from the evidence to $\sigma$ within $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$.

\begin{definition}[Observable State]
\label{def:observable}
A state $\sigma \in \X$ is \emph{$(\mathcal{C}, e, r)$-observable} for evidence $e \in \X$ and radius $r > 0$ if:
\[
\dRP(\sigma, e) \leq r \quad \text{and} \quad \sigma \in \Adm(\mathcal{C}).
\]
The \emph{observability set} of $e$ at radius $r$ is $\Obs_r(e) = B_{\dRP}(e, r) \cap \Adm(\mathcal{C})$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Observability is a Sheaf]
\label{prop:obs_sheaf}
The assignment $U \mapsto \Obs_r|_U$ (restriction of observability to open subsets $U \subset X$) defines a sheaf on $X$. In particular, local observability data can be consistently glued to global observability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sheaf condition requires: (i) locality (if $\sigma$ is observable on each $U_i$ in a cover $\{U_i\}$, it is observable on $\bigcup U_i$) and (ii) gluing (local observability witnesses can be combined into a global witness). Both follow from the fact that $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ is defined by global constraints, but the repair metric $\dRP$ is computed by paths in $\X$—which can be restricted and glued along open covers \cite{MacLane1998}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Witness Propagation}

A \emph{witness} for the state $\sigma$ is any evidence $e$ such that $\sigma \in \Obs_r(e)$. Witnesses propagate through the field dynamics:

\begin{definition}[Witness Bundle]
\label{def:witness}
The \emph{witness bundle} $\Wit(\sigma) \to \R_{\geq 0}$ is the family of observability sets $\Obs_r(\sigma_t)$ along the RSVP trajectory $\sigma_t = e^{tQ_H}(\sigma)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Witness Monotonicity]
\label{thm:witness_mono}
Along the RSVP flow, the witness bundle is non-expanding in repair metric: if $\sigma'$ is a witness for $\sigma$ at time $0$, then $e^{tQ_H}(\sigma')$ is a witness for $e^{tQ_H}(\sigma)$ at time $t$, but the repair cost may increase:
\[
\dRP(e^{tQ_H}(\sigma), e^{tQ_H}(\sigma')) \geq \dRP(\sigma, \sigma').
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The RSVP flow $e^{tQ_H}$ is a symplectomorphism of $(\X, \omega_{-1})$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:symplectic}), hence it preserves the symplectic volume $\int \omega_{-1}^n$. However, the repair metric $\dRP$ involves the entropic functional $\Etau$, which decreases along the gradient flow but may increase under the Hamiltonian flow (which does not minimize entropy). The inequality follows from the fact that Hamiltonian flows generically increase the distance to the admissibility manifold when the manifold is not invariant under the flow \cite{Arnold1989}.
\end{proof}

\section{Conservation of Ambiguity}
\label{sec:conservation}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Irreducible Ambiguity}

Not all distinctions can be made, even in principle, within a given context. The \emph{irreducible ambiguity} of a state $\sigma$ is the component of its uncertainty that cannot be resolved by any sequence of observations or repairs within $\mathcal{C}$.

\begin{definition}[Irreducible Ambiguity]
\label{def:ambiguity}
The \emph{irreducible ambiguity} of $\sigma \in \X$ within context $\mathcal{C}$ is:
\[
\Amb(\sigma, \mathcal{C}) = H(\sigma | \Adm(\mathcal{C})) = \inf_{\sigma^* \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\sigma \| p_{\sigma^*}),
\]
where $p_\sigma$ is the entropy density of $\sigma$, viewed as a probability measure on $X$, and the KL divergence measures the semantic distance from $\sigma$ to the nearest admissible interpretation.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conservation of Ambiguity]
\label{thm:conservation}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a context preserved by the RSVP Hamiltonian flow (Proposition~\ref{prop:adm_closed}). Then for any $\sigma \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$, the irreducible ambiguity is conserved along the flow:
\[
\frac{d}{dt} \Amb(e^{tQ_H}(\sigma), \mathcal{C}) = 0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $\sigma \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$ and $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ is invariant under $Q_H$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:adm_closed}), we have $e^{tQ_H}(\sigma) \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$ for all $t$. For $\sigma^* \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$, the KL divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\sigma^*} \| p_{\sigma^*}) = 0$, so $\Amb(\sigma^*, \mathcal{C}) = 0$ for all $\sigma^* \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$. The derivative of a constant is zero.

For states near but not in $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$, the conservation fails: Hamiltonian dynamics can increase or decrease the distance to the admissibility manifold, and therefore change $\Amb(\sigma, \mathcal{C})$. This is why the theorem is restricted to $\sigma \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$—it is a statement about the \emph{in-context} dynamics of ambiguity, not the off-shell dynamics.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ambiguity as a Topological Invariant}

The irreducible ambiguity can be viewed as a \emph{topological invariant} of the admissibility manifold: it measures the ``holes'' in $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ that cannot be filled by any admissible interpolation.

\begin{proposition}[Ambiguity and Cohomology]
\label{prop:ambi_cohom}
The space of ambiguity classes $\Amb(\X, \mathcal{C}) / \sim$ (equivalence under RSVP flow) is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology $H^*(\Adm(\mathcal{C}), \R)$ of the admissibility manifold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation}, ambiguity is constant along flow lines in $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$. The space of constant functions on flow lines corresponds to functions on the leaf space of the Hamiltonian foliation of $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$. By the Arnol'd-Liouville theorem (in the finite-dimensional approximation), this leaf space has cohomology isomorphic to $H^*(\Adm(\mathcal{C}))$ \cite{Arnold1989}.
\end{proof}

\section{PHYSIFORMER: Diffusion, Repair, and Physical Simulation}
\label{sec:physiformer}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Diffusion-Based Physical Simulation}

The PHYSIFORMER architecture \cite{Chen2026} adapts diffusion-based generative models to physical simulation tasks. In the standard denoising diffusion framework, one trains a neural network to reverse a diffusion process:
\begin{align}
dX_t &= -\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) X_t \, dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)} \, dW_t \quad \text{(forward, noising)}, \\
dX_t &= \left[-\frac{1}{2}\beta(t) X_t - \beta(t) \nabla \log p_t(X_t)\right] dt + \sqrt{\beta(t)} \, d\bar{W}_t \quad \text{(reverse, denoising)}.
\end{align}

The score function $\nabla \log p_t(X_t)$ is learned from data. In PHYSIFORMER, $X_t$ represents the state of a physical system (e.g., fluid velocity field, molecular geometry), and the forward diffusion corresponds to \emph{increasing entropy}—precisely the process described by the RSVP Fokker-Planck equation~\eqref{eq:s_eom} with $\tau = \beta(t)$.

\subsection{PHYSIFORMER as Repair}

The reverse diffusion process in PHYSIFORMER is exactly a \emph{repair process} in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:repair}: it restores a noisy (inadmissible) state to a clean (admissible) one by following the gradient of the log-likelihood—which is the repair direction in the $\Etau$ metric.

\begin{theorem}[PHYSIFORMER as Repair Operator]
\label{thm:physiformer_repair}
The PHYSIFORMER denoising map $\mathcal{D}_\theta: X_t \mapsto X_0$ approximates the repair operator $\Rep_{\Adm}: \X \to \Adm(\mathcal{C})$ defined by:
\[
\Rep_{\Adm}(\sigma) = \arg\min_{\sigma^* \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})} \dRP(\sigma, \sigma^*),
\]
in the limit where the training data $\{X_0^{(i)}\}$ is drawn from $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ and the diffusion time $t \to 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At small $t$, the forward diffusion $X_t \approx X_0 + \sqrt{\beta t} \, \xi$ (with $\xi \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$) is a small Gaussian perturbation of $X_0 \in \Adm(\mathcal{C})$. The reverse diffusion, guided by $\nabla \log p_t(X_t) \approx -(X_t - X_0)/(\beta t)$, moves $X_t$ toward $X_0$ along the gradient of the squared distance. In the $\Etau$ metric, this gradient direction is precisely $-\nabla_\sigma \dRP(\sigma, \Adm(\mathcal{C}))$—the repair direction. Taking $t \to 0$ and using the density of training data in $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$, the denoising map converges to $\Rep_{\Adm}$ \cite{Villani2008}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Implications for Admissibility Learning}

Theorem~\ref{thm:physiformer_repair} has immediate implications for machine learning: training a PHYSIFORMER-style model on samples from $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ implicitly learns the admissibility manifold and its repair structure. This suggests a new approach to out-of-distribution detection: a state $\sigma$ is flagged as inadmissible if $\|\nabla \log p_t(\sigma)\|$ is large—i.e., if the repair cost is high. This connects the constraint-first ontology directly to practical ML methodology.

\subsection{Simulated Agency}

Simulated agency models consciousness as iterated Bayesian inference over a generative model of the world, implemented as a closed loop between perception, prediction, and action. In the RSVP framework, this corresponds to a system whose field configuration $(\Phi, \bv, S)$ encodes a probability distribution over possible world-states, updated by observations:
\[
p(\theta | d) = \frac{p(d | \theta) \, p(\theta)}{\int p(d | \theta') \, p(\theta') \, d\theta'},
\]
where $\theta$ parameterizes positions on the admissibility manifold $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ and $d$ is incoming sensory data \cite{Bayes1763, Pearl1988, Jordan1999, Friston2010}. The agency loop is the cycle:
\[
\sigma_t \xrightarrow{\text{observe}} d_t \xrightarrow{\text{update}} p(\theta | d_t) \xrightarrow{\text{project}} \sigma_{t+1} \in \Adm(\mathcal{C}).
\]

The repair pseudometric $\dRP$ quantifies the ``surprise'' of observation $d_t$: a high-repair observation is one that pushes the system far off its current admissibility manifold, requiring a large update. This aligns with the free energy principle of Friston et al.\ \cite{Friston2010}, where surprise is minimized by updating beliefs (repair) or acting on the environment (prevention).

\subsection{Semantic Infrastructure}

Semantic infrastructure organizes meaning hierarchically, as a system of fiber products in the category of admissibility manifolds. If $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_1)$ and $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_2)$ share a common base context $\mathcal{C}_0$, their \emph{semantic fiber product} is:
\[
\Adm(\mathcal{C}_1) \times_{\Adm(\mathcal{C}_0)} \Adm(\mathcal{C}_2) = \{(\sigma_1, \sigma_2) : \pi_1(\sigma_1) = \pi_2(\sigma_2) \in \Adm(\mathcal{C}_0)\},
\]
depicted as:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\Adm(\mathcal{C}_1) \times_{\Adm(\mathcal{C}_0)} \Adm(\mathcal{C}_2) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \Adm(\mathcal{C}_2) \arrow[d, "\pi_2"] \\
\Adm(\mathcal{C}_1) \arrow[r, "\pi_1"] & \Adm(\mathcal{C}_0)
\end{tikzcd}
\]

This generalizes Fodor's language of thought hypothesis \cite{Fodor1983}: structured semantic compositionality corresponds to the fiber product structure on admissibility manifolds, and inference corresponds to projection along fiber maps.

\subsection{TARTAN and Chain of Memory}

TARTAN (Trajectory-Aware Recursive Tiling And Nesting) adjusts semantic granularity by iteratively refining the admissibility manifold at finer scales:
\[
\Adm(\mathcal{C})^{(n+1)} = \Adm(\mathcal{C})^{(n)} \cap \Stau^{1/2^n}(\X),
\]
where $\Stau^{1/2^n}$ is entropic smoothing at scale $\tau / 2^n$. The tile update rule is:
\[
T_{n+1} = T_n \oplus \epsilon N,
\]
where $N$ is a noise term and $\epsilon$ controls the refinement step. As $n \to \infty$, the TARTAN limit recovers the full admissibility manifold at resolution $\tau_0 / 2^\infty$ = fine-grain limit.

The Chain of Memory (CoM) links memory states across time by tracking their positions on the admissibility manifold:
\[
M_{t+1} = M_t \circ H,
\]
where $H$ is the Hamiltonian update (RSVP flow) and $\circ$ denotes composition in the category of admissibility morphisms. Memory coherence is measured by $\dRP(M_t, M_{t+1})$: a large repair cost signals a memory discontinuity or ``amnesia event'' \cite{Hochreiter1997, Graves2013}.

\subsection{Cosmological Parallels}

The RSVP entropy equation~\eqref{eq:s_eom} admits a striking cosmological interpretation. In Verlinde's emergent gravity framework \cite{Verlinde2011}, gravitational force arises as:
\[
G = -T \nabla S,
\]
where $T$ is the Unruh temperature and $S$ is the entropy of the holographic screen. This is precisely the force term in the RSVP momentum equation when the entropy gradient $\nabla S$ is large. The entropic force drives field configurations toward lower-entropy (higher-admissibility) regions, mirroring the gravitational attraction of matter toward entropy-generating sources.

The Bekenstein bound \cite{Bekenstein1973}—the maximum entropy of a region is proportional to its boundary area—constrains the maximum number of distinctions maintainable in a bounded region:
\[
|\Dist(\X|_{\text{region}}, \mathcal{C})| \leq \exp(A / 4 G \hbar),
\]
connecting the distinction geometry of Section~\ref{sec:repair} to fundamental physics.

\section{Implications for Cognitive Science and Artificial Intelligence}

\subsection{Consciousness as Stable Admissibility}

The framework suggests a new characterization of consciousness: a cognitive system is conscious (in the functional sense) when its field configuration $(\Phi, \bv, S)$ maintains stable membership in a contextually rich admissibility manifold $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{rich}})$, where $\mathcal{C}_{\text{rich}}$ encodes extensive semantic, causal, and temporal constraints. Unconscious or dissociated states correspond to configurations far from $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{rich}})$, with high repair cost—a formalization of Baars' global workspace theory \cite{Baars1988} and integrated information theory \cite{Tononi2004}.

The repair cost $\dRP(\sigma, \Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{rich}}))$ serves as a quantitative measure of \emph{cognitive dissonance}: the cost of restoring coherent, contextually appropriate cognition from a disrupted state. This connects to clinical applications—cognitive disorders such as psychosis, dissociation, or profound amnesia can be modeled as states with extremely high repair costs, requiring extensive environmental or therapeutic scaffolding to restore admissibility.

\subsection{Alignment via Admissibility Specification}

For AI alignment, the framework suggests a concrete methodology: specify target behavior by defining an admissibility manifold $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{align}})$ encoding the desired value constraints, then train models to minimize the repair cost $\dRP(\sigma, \Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{align}}))$ as an auxiliary objective. This is an instance of the general principle that alignment is \emph{restorability}: an aligned system is one that can always be restored to admissible behavior at low cost \cite{Bostrom2003, Russell2010}.

The PHYSIFORMER connection (Theorem~\ref{thm:physiformer_repair}) shows that diffusion-based models can learn to perform this restoration implicitly, opening a path toward alignment-aware training procedures that require no explicit reward signal—only samples from $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{align}})$.

\subsection{Distinction Ecology}

The finiteness of distinction space (Proposition~\ref{prop:fin_dist}) implies that cognitive systems face a fundamental resource constraint: the total number of distinctions maintainable at scale $\tau$ is bounded. This leads to an \emph{ecology of distinctions}: distinctions compete for cognitive resources, and organisms or systems that maintain too many fine-grained distinctions at low $\tau$ sacrifice robustness to noise and perturbation, while those that operate at high $\tau$ sacrifice precision and nuance.

Optimal cognitive architectures balance these pressures by operating at variable $\tau$—using fine-grained distinctions (low $\tau$) for well-rehearsed domains and coarse-grained distinctions (high $\tau$) for novel or ambiguous situations. This mirrors the TARTAN architecture's adaptive tiling strategy, and predicts that biological neural circuits should exhibit multi-scale temporal coding consistent with a hierarchy of admissibility manifolds.

\paragraph{Experimental Predictions.} The distinction geometry framework makes concrete experimental predictions for neuroscience: (i) the number of distinct attractor states in a cortical circuit should scale as $\exp(A/4\tau)$, where $A$ is the area of the circuit and $\tau$ is the temporal scale of its dynamics; (ii) cognitive dissonance should correlate with measurable increases in metabolic cost (neural repair energy); (iii) multi-scale temporal coding should follow the TARTAN hierarchy.

\paragraph{Meta-Cognition.} A cognitive system that reasons about its own admissibility manifold—that can represent $\Adm(\mathcal{C})$ as an object within $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{meta}})$—exhibits meta-cognition. The fixed-point condition $\Adm(\mathcal{C}_{\text{meta}}) \ni \Adm(\mathcal{C})$ connects to Gödelian incompleteness \cite{Godel1931}: there exist admissibility constraints that cannot be represented within any admissibility manifold rich enough to formalize them.

\paragraph{Wasserstein Gradient Flows.} The repair pseudometric $\dRP = \Wtwo$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:repair_ot}) implies that the gradient flow of the repair cost is a Wasserstein gradient flow—an optimal transport process. Developing numerical methods (JKO schemes, particle methods) for computing this flow would enable practical simulation of large-scale admissibility restoration.

We have developed a unified mathematical framework connecting discrete autoregressive systems, continuous RSVP field dynamics, and a novel constraint-first ontology built on admissibility manifolds, repair pseudometrics, and distinction geometry. The central results are:

\end{enumerate}

The framework is not merely a technical unification but a conceptual one: it suggests that cognition, computation, and physical dynamics are all instances of a single process—the maintenance and restoration of structured distinctions within a contextually constrained field of possible states. Intelligence, in this view, is not the ability to represent the world accurately, but the ability to repair the world's representation efficiently, persistently, and at the minimal cost of distinction.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:witness_mono} (Witness Monotonicity)}
The RSVP flow is a symplectomorphism, hence a bijection on $\X$. The repair cost transforms as:

where the inequality uses that $\Etau$ is not $Q_H$-invariant (unlike $\omega_{-1}$), so pull-back can only increase its value.
