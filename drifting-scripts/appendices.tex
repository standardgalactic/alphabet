\section{Counting Derivations}\label{app:counting}

\paragraph{Involutions.}
Let $I(n)$ be the number of involutions on $\{1,\dots,n\}$. Classify an involution by what it does to the element $n$. If $n$ is fixed, the rest is an involution on $n-1$ elements, giving $I(n-1)$ possibilities. If $n$ is exchanged with $j\ne n$, there are $n-1$ choices of $j$ and the remaining $n-2$ elements carry an involution, giving $(n-1)I(n-2)$. Hence $I(n)=I(n-1)+(n-1)I(n-2)$ with $I(0)=I(1)=1$. Iterating to $n=26$ gives $I(26)=532{,}985{,}208{,}200{,}576$.

\paragraph{Involutions with exactly $k$ exchanges.}
Choose the $2k$ moved letters in $\binom{26}{2k}$ ways. A fixed-point-free involution on $2k$ letters is a perfect matching; the number of these is $(2k-1)!!$, since the smallest letter can be matched with any of $2k-1$ others and the remaining $2k-2$ letters matched in $(2k-3)!!$ ways. Summing $\binom{26}{2k}(2k-1)!!$ over $k=0,\dots,13$ reproduces $I(26)$ exactly, which was checked by computer. The values for $k=4,6,7,9,11,12$, omitted from Table~\ref{tab:counting}, are $164{,}038{,}875$; $100{,}391{,}791{,}500$; $1{,}305{,}093{,}289{,}500$; $53{,}835{,}098{,}191{,}875$; $205{,}552{,}193{,}096{,}250$; and $102{,}776{,}096{,}548{,}125$. The largest single term is at $k=11$, so most reciprocal alphabets change 22 of the 26 letters.

\paragraph{Derangements.}
The number $D(n)$ of permutations of $n$ elements without fixed points satisfies $D(n)=(n-1)\bigl(D(n-1)+D(n-2)\bigr)$ with $D(0)=1$ and $D(1)=0$, and $D(26)=148{,}362{,}637{,}348{,}470{,}135{,}821{,}287{,}825$. The ratio $26!/I(26)\approx7.57\times10^{11}$.

\paragraph{Exposure-driven staging.}
The script below assigns to each occurrence of an alphabet letter a stage from $0$ to $S$, advancing one stage after each $K$ further occurrences once a letter-specific delay has passed. Run on the text of the working conversation from which this essay was developed (about 9{,}700 letters) with $K=5$ and $S=3$ and the pairs B/P, U/V, C/G, E/F, M/N and O/Q, it reports the character positions at which each of the twelve letters first reaches its final stage: E at 152, O at 247, N at 409, C at 444, U at 496, P at 602, B at 744, M at 762, G at 921, V at 1647, F at 2449 and Q at 8134. The order follows letter frequency, and the rare letter Q, with 21 occurrences in the text, reaches its final stage only after 8{,}134 characters.

\section{A Schema for Edition Manifests}\label{app:manifest}

The following schema lists the fields an edition manifest would carry. It is a specification and not a record of any existing edition, and the values shown are symbolic.

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
edition:
  id:          <hash of the fields below>
  kind:        authorial | experimental | derivative
  text:
    work:      <identifier of the work>
    version:   <version of the linguistic text>
    parent:    <identifier of the preceding version, if any>
  alphabet:    <identifier, e.g. Latin capitals and lower case>
  substitution:
    table:     <identifier of the bijection pi>
    reciprocal: true | false
  glyphs:
    masters:   <identifiers of the master outlines>
    routes:    <per-letter route definitions, endpoints and keyframes>
    license:   <licence of the outlines>
  schedule:
    kind:      uniform | exposure-driven | letter-by-letter | cyclic
    parameters: {K, S, delays D_l, novelty cap nu}
    seed:      <recorded if any randomness is used>
  margin:
    threshold: theta
    certificate: <result of the build-time check>
  builder:
    version:   <version of the edition builder>
  evidence:    <links to observations made on this edition, if any>
\end{lstlisting}
