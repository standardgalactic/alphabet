We live in a time of accelerating confusion. We grapple with the challenge of aligning artificial intelligence, navigate the disorienting dynamics of social media, and still ponder the fundamental mysteries of our own consciousness. These problems feel vast, complex, and, above all, **separate**.

But what if they aren't? What if a single, underlying framework—a kind of **physics for meaning itself**—could connect them all?

This post will distill the five most surprising and impactful takeaways from this new *"physics of thought,"* revealing the hidden laws that govern our digital and mental worlds.

## 1. Thinking Isn't Magic. It's a Physical Process With a Strict Budget.

The theory begins with a foundational claim:

> **Reasoning, agency, and even a single thought are not abstract computations but physical processes unfolding in a "semantic plenum"—a field of meaning.**

This moves cognition from the realm of **software** into the realm of **physics**, with all the constraints that implies.

Thought operates under two strict budgets:

- **Sparsity** — Every cognitive operation has a metabolic cost. Neurons firing, concepts activating: all of it consumes energy. Efficient thought must therefore use the **smallest possible number of components** to achieve its goal. This is mathematically formalized using a *LASSO-style objective* that enforces parsimony.
- **Entropy** — There is a limit to how much noise, disorder, or incoherence a thought process can survive before dissolving into randomness.

This reframes *bounded rationality*:

> It isn't a psychological flaw—it's a **law of cognitive thermodynamics**.

Our brains aren't lazy. They're **forced to be sparse and efficient**, because the cost of thinking is real.

## 2. Social Media Obeys Laws of Extraction (and It's Making You Work Harder for Less)

The feeling of posting into the void—more effort, worse reach—can be expressed using the same physics.

Extractive platforms operate using three fields:

- **Visibility Potential (Φ)** — your ability to be seen
- **Agency Vector (v)** — your effort (posting, creating, engaging)
- **Informational Entropy (S)** — chaos and competition in the feed

These obey two structural laws:

```

∇Φ · v < 0   →   More effort leads to less visibility
∇S · v > 0   →   More effort increases system chaos

```

Why?

Because every post you make:

1. Adds to the total noise of the system
2. Increases competition for attention
3. Makes it harder for *any* signal to stand out—including your own

This dynamic is not personal. It is **thermodynamic**.

## 3. AI Can Be Right for the Wrong Reasons

Two AI models can produce the **same output** with **radically different internal representations**.

- **SGD-trained models** often learn *entangled*, chaotic internal structures—"spaghetti representations" that work but don’t generalize.
- **Evolutionary or open-ended processes** discover *factored*, interpretable structures—modular and reusable representations that reflect true causal structure.

Example:

> One model represents a skull as tangled numerical correlations.  
> Another represents it with discrete knobs for *jaw width*, *eye spacing*, *symmetry axes*, etc.

Both draw the skull. Only one **understands** it.

This leads to the key insight:

> *“Two LLMs may behave identically on the outside, but internally one may have a coherent world-model and the other a broken one.”*
