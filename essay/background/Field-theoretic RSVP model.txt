Here's an outline of Robin Hanson's futarchy concept:

## Core Concept
Futarchy is a proposed form of governance that combines democratic values with market mechanisms. The slogan is "Vote values, but bet beliefs" - citizens democratically choose what outcomes to optimize for, while prediction markets determine which policies will best achieve those outcomes.

## How It Works

**Democratic Component:**
- Citizens vote on welfare functions or values (what society should optimize for)
- Examples: GDP growth, happiness indices, environmental quality, crime reduction

**Market Component:**
- For each proposed policy, conditional prediction markets are created
- Markets predict the chosen welfare measure conditional on policy adoption vs. rejection
- The policy is implemented if markets show it would improve the welfare measure

## Market Mechanism
- Traders bet on outcomes like "GDP in 2030 if Policy X is adopted"
- Market prices aggregate information and reveal expected policy impacts
- Policies with higher predicted welfare outcomes are automatically adopted
- Markets are "killed" (bets refunded) for the path not taken

## Theoretical Advantages
- Separates values (democratic input) from beliefs about cause-and-effect (market expertise)
- Harnesses financial incentives to reveal accurate information
- Reduces influence of special interests and uninformed voters on technical policy questions
- Creates incentives for research into policy effectiveness

## Challenges and Criticisms
- Requires liquid, accurate prediction markets on complex social outcomes
- Difficult to define and measure welfare functions
- May concentrate political power among wealthy traders
- Questions about market manipulation and gaming
- Untested at scale in real governance systems

Hanson developed this concept in the early 2000s, arguing it could improve on both democracy and technocracy by leveraging market efficiency while maintaining democratic legitimacy.
