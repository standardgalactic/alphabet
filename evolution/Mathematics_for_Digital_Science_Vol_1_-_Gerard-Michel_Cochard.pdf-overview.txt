3. **Antisymmetry**: A relation is antisymmetric if for any distinct elements \( x \) and \( y \), whenever both \( (x, y) \) and \( (y, x) \) are in the relation, then \( x \) must equal \( y \). For example, "being a square of" or "less than or equal to" is antisymmetric.

4. **Transitivity**: A relationship is transitive if whenever an element \( x \) relates to \( y \), and \( y \) relates to \( z \), then \( x \) also relates to \( z \). For instance, "being greater than or equal to" is transitive.

5. **Equivalence Relations**: An equivalence relation satisfies reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity simultaneously. Examples include:
   - Equality among real numbers.
   - Parallelism between lines in space.

6. **Equivalence Classes**: Given an equivalence relation on a set \( E \), the equivalence class of an element \( a \) is the set of all elements related to \( a \). Equivalence classes partition the set \( E \).
