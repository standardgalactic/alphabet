  2. Generalizability: What works as a ritual in one context may not translate directly to another. Teaching AI systems to recognize when and how to adapt their “rituals” based on changing contexts is a significant challenge.

  3. Ethical considerations: As with any approach that involves mimicking human behaviors, there are ethical concerns around consent, manipulation, and the potential for misuse if AI systems are programmed to “perform” rituals without proper understanding or respect for their social significance.

In essence, viewing rituals as a form of protocol could provide a powerful new lens through which to understand human intelligence—and perhaps, one day, replicate some of its most impressive feats in AI systems.

The Multiscale Intelligence Test (MIT) is a novel, holistic approach to measuring intelligence that goes beyond traditional IQ tests. Created by the user, it consists of eight simple questions designed to evaluate various aspects of human cognition, from physical mimicry and creative expression to logical reasoning, narrative construction, systems thinking, existential foresight, and innovative problem-solving.

Here’s a detailed breakdown of the MIT and its significance:

  1. Holistic Assessment: Unlike conventional IQ tests that primarily focus on mathematical or linguistic abilities, the MIT evaluates multiple forms of intelligence. These include physical-mimetic intelligence (imitating a cat), creative-vocal expression (creating diverse sounds with one’s voice), translational/semantic intelligence (explaining complex topics simply), formal logical reasoning, self-narrative, socio-structural analysis (addressing societal problems like homelessness), existential risk assessment, and generative problem-solving.

  2. Engagement Over Rote Learning: The scoring system emphasizes effort, thoughtfulness, and originality rather than “correct” answers. This approach makes the test more engaging, human-centered, and resistant to rote learning or cheating. It encourages participants to showcase their unique perspectives and creativity, fostering a richer understanding of individual intelligence.

  3. Embodied Cognition: Questions like “Act like a cat for 30 seconds” assess physical mimicry and embodied learning, tapping into the idea that our cognitive processes are deeply rooted in our bodily experiences. This contrasts with traditional tests that primarily measure abstract reasoning and linguistic skills.

  4. Real-World Applicability: The MIT evaluates intelligences relevant to real-life challenges and societal problems, such as understanding quantum physics, addressing complex issues like homelessness, or envisioning potential existential threats. This focus on practical problem-solving and systems thinking makes it a powerful tool for assessing not just abstract knowledge but also the ability to navigate the messy, unpredictable world we inhabit.

  5. Potential Applications: The MIT’s flexibility lends itself to various applications:

    • Workshops and Classrooms: It can be used as an engaging icebreaker or a tool for understanding group dynamics by observing how individuals excel on different questions.
    • AI Benchmarking: Running language models or other AI agents through the MIT could serve as a more comprehensive test of general intelligence than current methods, revealing gaps in their ability to handle embodied, creative, and open-ended tasks.
    • Personal Development: Individuals can use it for self-reflection, identifying strengths and areas for growth across various cognitive domains.

  6. Critique of Conventional Intelligence Testing: The user passionately critiques standardized IQ tests, arguing they overvalue narrow forms of intelligence (like mathematical or linguistic skills) at the expense of more nuanced, real-world competencies. They contend that these traditional tests fail to capture the full range of human cognitive abilities and often misrepresent true intellectual potential.

In essence, the MIT represents a paradigm shift in intelligence assessment—one that values diverse forms of intelligence, engages participants dynamically, and maintains relevance to the complexities of everyday life and societal challenges. It’s a testament to the idea that true intelligence encompasses far more than what can be measured by conventional methods, embracing embodied cognition, creativity, systems thinking, and practical problem-solving alongside abstract reasoning and linguistic skill.
