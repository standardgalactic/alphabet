## The Mystery of Why Things Stay Put

The history of physics is a sequence of ontological compressions. For centuries, physical theory operated within a Newtonian dynamical grammar that treated reality as matter evolving inside a pre-existing spatial container. In this picture, the universe resembles a machine gradually exhausting its usable structure. The second law of thermodynamics appears to guarantee that every organized system—from galaxies to biological minds—is a temporary fluctuation destined to dissolve into equilibrium.

Yet the observable universe displays something stranger. Structure does not merely survive accidentally; it persists with a remarkable self-organizing resilience. Galaxies stabilize, ecosystems self-regulate, and minds maintain coherent continuity across time. This persistence suggests a deeper ontological inversion.

Perhaps the universe is not fundamentally a collection of objects contained within space, but a shifting network of evolving relational configurations. In this interpretation, relations and transformations are primary, while space, time, and law emerge from deeper organizational constraints.

The Relativistic Scalar-Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework extends this inversion by proposing that the universe is governed not by externally imposed laws but by recursive admissibility. Reality persists because certain configurations remain recursively maintainable across scales.

The laws of physics become not commands imposed from outside the universe, but stabilized survivors of an ongoing process of admissibility filtering.
