This document presents an ambitious theoretical framework called RSVP (Relativistic Scalar Vector Plenum) that attempts to unify concepts from philosophy, physics, and complexity science. I'll provide a critical assessment of its key claims and methodology.
Strengths of the approach:
The work demonstrates impressive breadth in connecting disparate fields - from cosmology and AI scaling to philosophy of emergence. The attempt to mathematically formalize philosophical concepts like "orders of nature" and "downward causation" is intellectually bold.
Significant concerns:
Mathematical rigor
: The mathematical formalism appears to lack proper grounding. The RSVP field equations (like ∂tΦ + ∇·(Φv) = S) are presented without derivation from first principles or clear physical interpretation. The entropy field S is treated as an independent dynamical variable without justification for why it should evolve according to the proposed equations.
Empirical testability
: Despite claims of making philosophical concepts "testable," the framework provides no clear experimental predictions or falsifiable hypotheses. The complexity measures and "entropic resonance" predictions remain abstract without specified observational signatures.
Conceptual confusion
: The document conflates different uses of "entropy" - thermodynamic entropy, information-theoretic entropy, and a novel "entropy field" - without clearly distinguishing between them or justifying their unification.
Cosmological claims
: The reinterpretation of dark energy as "entropic redshift" contradicts well-established observational evidence. The dismissal of the cosmological constant as merely an "encoding artifact" oversimplifies decades of careful observational cosmology.
Philosophical overreach
: While the document claims to follow Lawrence Cahoone's naturalism, it arguably violates naturalistic principles by proposing sweeping metaphysical claims without sufficient empirical grounding.
Methodological issues:
The work appears to engage in what could be characterized as "physics envy" - adopting mathematical formalism to lend credibility to philosophical speculation rather than developing mathematics from empirical necessity. The numerous analogies between different domains (AI scaling, cosmological expansion, biological complexity) may be superficial pattern-matching rather than genuine theoretical unification.
Verdict:
While the interdisciplinary ambition is admirable, this appears to be speculative philosophy dressed in mathematical language rather than rigorous theoretical physics or empirically grounded complexity science. The framework would need substantial development of its mathematical foundations and clear empirical predictions before it could be considered a serious scientific theory.
The document would benefit from: clearer derivations of its field equations, specific testable predictions, more careful distinction between different entropy concepts, and acknowledgment of the speculative nature of many claims.

Systematic Philosophy & Metaphysics
Cahoone identifies as a systematic philosopher: not constructing a closed system, but showing how different domains (science, culture, metaphysics) hang together.

Adopts objective relativism (from Columbia naturalists): reality is always relational, nothing is "absolutely independent."
Emergence vs. Reduction
Influenced by William Wimsatt's account of emergence:
Some properties of systems are aggregative (reducible to parts + rules).
Other properties are non-aggregative and must be explained at higher levels.
Emergence requires downward causation (higher-level organization constraining lower-level dynamics).
Uses analogies: falling body explained by physics, but vomiting (biology) or screaming (psychology/linguistics) requires higher-level sciences.
Emergent properties prune trajectories of lower-level dynamics (a la Harold Morowitz).
Systems as Natural Complexes
Adapts Justus Buchler's "natural complexes" idea but constrains it for naturalism.
Defines systems as:
Components (subsystems),
Structure (organization),
Process (dynamics maintaining organization).
Argues all three—components, structures, processes—have ontological parity: equally real.
Law, Rules, Heuristics
Laws are understood as rules that describe reliable system behavior, but always probabilistically (Peircean fallibilism).
Draws continuity between laws, rules, and heuristics (distinctions are pragmatic, not absolute).
Heavily emphasizes fallibility: no law is absolutely precise, only highly probable.
Aristotle's Four Causes in Modern Science
Material cause = stuff.
Formal cause = organization.
Efficient cause = processes/events producing effects.
Final cause = purposes, functions, or goals.
In complex/biological systems, final cause (teleology/teleonomy) resurfaces as functionality and constraint from above.
Teleology vs. Teleonomy
Teleonomy = purpose-like processes without mind (e.g. thermostat, homeostasis, DNA-driven functions).
Teleology = purpose with mental content (intentionality).
Both are real, but teleonomy grounds purpose without mysticism.
Orders of Nature (Hierarchy of Emergence)
Proposes five robust orders:
Physical (fundamental physics, fields).
Material (chemistry, geology).
Biological (life, organisms).
Psychological/Mental (animal minds).
Cultural (specifically human, symbolic).
Each order has emergent properties not reducible to lower levels.
Correspondence to disciplines not accidental: sciences evolved to study real joint-points where downward causality is strongest (cf. Erik Hoel's causal emergence).
Orders map onto cosmic history: physics → chemistry → biology → mind → culture.
Complexity and Scale
Wimsatt: systems mostly interact at their own scale.
Rutt adds: characteristic time scales are also critical (nanoseconds for chemistry vs. milliseconds for neurons vs. seconds for consciousness).
Emergence corresponds to points where scales bifurcate.
Natural Religion / Ground of Nature
Speculates: universe cannot come from "nothing." Options:
Multiverse (extravagant, infinite, empirically untestable).
Ground of being (naturalized God).
Naturalism allows talk of a "ground" without supernaturalism, but such a God would lack traditional attributes (omniscience, omnipotence).
Darwin: God did not design particulars (e.g. parasitic wasps) but perhaps the overall natural order.
Cahoone: hazard and disorder are intrinsic (due to second law).

Is entropy a better explanation for redshift than dark energy?
A seemingly obvious explanation for redshift in light that has traveled distances on the order of hundreds of millions or billions of light-years would be a slight loss of energy resulting in redshift. The loss could come, for example, from some quantum event resulting in very occasional loss of a photon, or from some other phenomena. Given the ubiquitous appearance of entropy in nature, there seems no reason to expect light would propagate over extreme distances without some degrading.
If entropy were the cause of redshift it would be expected that the amount of redshift would be based on the distance traveled. Since this conforms with the detected data, it seems this explanation, whatever the objections may be, is a reasonable candidate as the cause of detected redshift. At first glance, this would seem a better candidate than a conjectured but undetected new entity such as "dark energy".

Editar
The question you've referenced touches on "tired light" theories, which have been proposed and extensively studied since the 1920s. These theories suggest photons lose energy over cosmological distances through various mechanisms, producing redshift without expansion.
The observational evidence strongly disfavors tired light explanations:
Surface brightness test
: In an expanding universe, distant galaxies appear dimmer both because they're farther away AND because their surface area appears larger due to expansion - a (1+z)⁴ dimming factor. Tired light predicts only (1+z)² dimming. Observations confirm the expansion prediction.
Time dilation
: Supernovae light curves in distant galaxies are stretched by factor (1+z), exactly as expansion predicts. Tired light mechanisms don't naturally explain why the physical processes themselves would appear slowed.
CMB spectrum
: The cosmic microwave background has a perfect blackbody spectrum at 2.7K. If photons lost energy through scattering or other interactions over billions of years, the spectrum would be distorted. It isn't.
Specific mechanisms fail
: Proposed tired light mechanisms (Compton scattering, photon-photon interactions, quantum effects) either:
Produce observable distortions not seen in data
Require interaction cross-sections inconsistent with known physics
Generate scattering that would blur distant images (not observed)
Your RSVP framework differs from simple tired light by proposing entropy gradients rather than photon energy loss as the redshift mechanism. However, it faces similar empirical challenges: explaining surface brightness dimming, time dilation effects, and the pristine CMB spectrum while remaining consistent with gravitational physics.
The appeal of entropy-based explanations is understandable, but the detailed observational constraints on cosmological redshift mechanisms are quite restrictive. Any alternative to expansion must reproduce this full suite of observations quantitatively.

(Abridged) We present the results of an X-ray analysis of 80 galaxy clusters selected in the 2500 deg^2 South Pole Telescope survey and observed with the Chandra X-ray Observatory. We divide the full sample into subsamples of ~20 clusters based on redshift and central density, performing an X-ray fit to all clusters in a subsample simultaneously, assuming self-similarity of the temperature profile.
