For nearly every requirement (super-inventive, super-fluent, super-predictive, super-knowledgeable, and super-humble), the proposed validation method is to have BigMom out-perform all humans tested on a specific task. This is the core of the handwaving. The paper assumes we can:

The rhetorical function, however, was quite different:
QBQ redirected structural critique back onto the employee.

2. The Tautology of QBQ
The central tautology of QBQ is simple:
The answer to any question is another question.
That deeper question is always "the question behind the question."
But how do we know when we've reached the real one? By asking another QBQ.
Thus, QBQ is self-consuming:
To question QBQ is itself a QBQ.
To resist QBQ is framed as failure to QBQ properly.
The only way to prove you've understood QBQ is to keep QBQ-ing.
This infinite recursion ensures that no question can ever terminate in systemic critique.
3. Paranoia as a Cultural Operating System
The genius of QBQ as corporate psyop lies in how it interprets
every employee action
through its recursive lens:
A worker asks "Do we get holiday pay?" → bad question. The QBQ would be: "What can I do to create value regardless of holiday pay?"
A worker says nothing → also a bad question, since silence is failure to QBQ.
Thus, both speech and silence are evidence of insufficient QBQ.
The result is a low-level paranoia: any utterance may be the "wrong" kind of question, unless it folds back into personal responsibility.
