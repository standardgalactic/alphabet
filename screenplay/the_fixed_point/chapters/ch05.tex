% The naming of the process. Others misunderstand immediately.
% Prayer as an inadequate but necessary word.

\chapter{The First Resolution}

\strandmarker{Guyon}{France, 1671}

\lettrine{S}{he} wrote it down, eventually.

Not the experience itself---she had already understood that the
experience did not survive transcription intact, that the act of
writing introduced the very structure she was trying to
describe the absence of. But she wrote the outline of it, the
negative space, a description shaped around what it was not.

She was not the first to notice that certain things resist direct
expression. But she had arrived at this understanding from a
direction she had not seen described, and that difference in
direction seemed important.

Those who had written of interior silence before her had
generally framed it as a discipline. A practice undertaken in
stages, with clear transitions, with criteria by which one could
know that progress was being made. The language implied effort,
even when what it described was the cessation of effort. Even
when it said: stop, the saying of it was a further movement.

What she wanted to say was simpler and harder.

That the stopping was not a step.

That it could not be added to the sequence as one more item.

That the sequence had to end, and after it ended, something else
became possible---but that something else could not be produced
by any additional operation.

\scenebreak

The word she had been given was prayer.

She used it, because there was no other word available, and
because abandoning language entirely was not an option she could
afford. She was writing to people who needed to be addressed,
who were waiting for some account of what she meant.

But she knew what the word did.

It arrived in her readers carrying everything they had already
been taught to associate with it: the address to a listener, the
shaping of desire into supplication, the expectation of response.
It implied a kind of conversation, a relation between two
parties, one of whom spoke and one of whom, eventually, answered.

What she had experienced was not a conversation.

It was, if anything, the opposite: the cessation of the
posture that made conversation possible. The dissolution of the
address. Not silence between two parties, but the temporary
ending of the distinction that produced parties in the first
place.

She wrote around this as carefully as she could. She added
qualifications. She introduced the word \textit{pure} in front of
prayer, hoping that the adjective would signal that what she
meant departed from the ordinary use. She wrote of abandonment,
of dispossession, of the will's own relinquishment of the will.

The readers understood that she meant something unusual.

They concluded that she meant something more intense than ordinary
prayer, rather than something structurally different from it.

This was not what she meant.

\scenebreak

The first person to read her account carefully was
F\'{e}nelon.

He came to it with a different kind of attention than she was
used to---not looking for what she had gotten wrong, nor
attempting to translate it into something already known, but
following the movement of what she was trying to say. He could
do this, she discovered, because he had encountered something
similar himself, though from a different direction and with less
clarity about its nature.

He asked her a question that no one else had asked.

Not what did you experience, nor is this compatible with
doctrine, but: where does it fail?

She had not expected the question. She sat with it for a moment
before answering.

It fails, she said, when it is mistaken for a method.

When it is added to the list of practices, assigned a duration,
given a time and a place in the day's ordering. When someone
attempts to produce the state by following a procedure designed
to produce the state.

He nodded. He understood what she meant, or came as close to
understanding it as she had yet encountered in another person.

Later, she would come to see that he understood it in the way
that someone understands a theorem they can follow but not
yet prove themselves: the shape of it was clear, the
implications were clear, but the interior of the thing, the
place from which it was self-evident, remained at a slight
remove.

He would spend years trying to close that remove.

He would come very close.

He would never quite arrive.

And this, she eventually understood, was not his failure.

It was the structure of the problem.
