% ── Chapter 6: The Broken Arm ─────────────────────────────────
% Opening of Part II. Mackandal. Saint-Domingue, early 1740s.
% Constraint shock. The body altered; perception sharpens.

\chapter{The Broken Arm}

\strandmarker{Mackandal}{Saint-Domingue, 1740}

\lettrine{T}{he} mill did not stop.

This was the first thing he understood afterward---not as a
thought, not yet, but as a sensation: the continuing sound of
the mechanism, the continued motion of the parts, the
indifference of the apparatus to what had just occurred within
it. One component had been damaged. The system had noted this
the way a current notes an obstruction, by flowing around it,
and continued.

He was carried away from it. He did not lose consciousness,
though he had been told, afterward, that this was unusual.
He remained aware throughout, which meant that he also remained
aware of the structure of what was happening to him---the
carrying, the weight of his own arm as something now external
to his will, the faces of the people around him calibrated to
expressions that would not invite punishment.

He catalogued these things. There was nothing else to do.

The plantation was a machine. He had known this before the
injury, but knowing it as a fact and knowing it through the
body were different orders of knowledge. Before, he had moved
within it---had been, in a technical sense, one of its working
parts. Now he was a broken part, and broken parts were examined
differently than functioning ones.

He understood, in the days that followed, that he was being
assessed.

Not as a person. As a component with diminished capacity.
The assessment was not cruel---it was worse than cruel, because
cruelty required acknowledgment of what was being damaged.
This was mechanical: what could he still do, what could no
longer be required of him, how should the remaining utility
be allocated.

He observed the assessment and did not speak.

\scenebreak

The arm healed partially. Enough to be functional in ways that
did not require the mill. He was assigned to the livestock,
which was understood by everyone involved as a demotion, and
which he understood as something else: a loosening of the
mechanism's grip.

The livestock moved. They moved according to their own
requirements, their own seasonality, their own networks of
need. Tending them required knowledge of a different kind
than the mill---not the fixed repetition of a mechanical
sequence, but attention to variation, to signal, to the small
differentials between one day and the next that indicated
whether the system was stable or approaching a threshold.

He was good at this. He did not know yet why.

He began to notice things he had not attended to before:
the way information moved across the plantation without being
visible as information. A change in someone's posture that was
read, by those nearby, as a message. A sound that was an
ordinary sound in one context and not ordinary in another.
The encoding was not written anywhere. It was distributed
across bodies and practices, maintained by repetition,
recoverable only by someone who had spent enough time inside
the system to feel when something was different.

He spent time inside the system.

He felt when something was different.

\scenebreak

The arm had taken something from him.

It had also, in a way he could not yet articulate, made a
different kind of attention possible. The person he had been
before the injury had existed inside the mechanism without
fully perceiving it, because the mechanism was the medium he
moved through, and media become invisible when they are
functioning correctly.

Damage had made the medium visible.

He could see, now, how the information flows worked. Where
they were permitted to go and where they were blocked. Which
channels carried weight and which carried only noise. The
structure of the thing, which had been everywhere around him
for years without being perceptible, had become---not legible
exactly, but detectable.

He began to pay attention in a different way.

Not to what was said, but to what was encoded in how it was
said. Not to the surface of what moved, but to what the
movement was carrying.

He did not yet know what to do with this.

He filed it, as one files things that have no immediate use,
against the possibility that a use might eventually present
itself.
