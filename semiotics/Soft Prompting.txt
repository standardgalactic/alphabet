Abstract
The performance of large language mod-
els in domain-specific tasks necessitates fine-
tuning, which is computationally expensive and
technically challenging. This paper focuses
on parameter-efficient fine-tuning using soft
prompting, a promising approach that adapts
pre-trained models to downstream tasks by
learning a small set of parameters. We pro-
pose a novel Input Dependent Soft Prompting
technique with a self-Attention Mechanism (ID-
SPAM) that generates soft prompts based on
the input tokens and attends different tokens
with varying importance. Our method is simple
and efficient, keeping the number of trainable
parameters small. We show the merits of the
proposed approach compared to state-of-the-
art techniques on various tasks and show the
improved zero shot domain transfer capability.
1 Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have made signifi-
cant advancements in natural language processing
tasks, such as generation, translation and summa-
rization (Yeo et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023a). De-
spite their success, LLMs' performance in domain-
specific tasks is limited, and fine-tuning on task-
oriented datasets is crucial. As models from BERT (Devlin et al., 2019) to GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020)
have millions to billions of parameters, fine-tuning
becomes computationally expensive and challeng-
ing. Therefore, parameter efficient fine-tuning (Han et al., 2024) research aims to adapt pre-trained
models to downstream tasks by fixing most param-
eters and only learning a small subset.
Soft prompting is a promising direction for fine-
tuning large models. Without changing the core
architecture of an LLM, soft prompt methods gen-
erally introduce a small trainable vector (known as
a 'soft prompt') at the beginning of one or more *Equal contribution. Work done during the internship at

fine tuning, only the soft prompt is trained to adapt
to the downstream task keeping the parameters of
the base LLM frozen. Lester et al.

Prompt Tuning by prepending the trainable soft

input, just after the embedding layer of the base

every transformer layer and Liu et al.
(2021) come

Recent literature introduces several variants of
soft prompt techniques such as removing the repa-

chical structured pruning (Ma et al., 2022), intro-

et al.
, 2023b), diving the soft prompt into query,
key and value prompts (Wang et al.
, 2023), learning
multiple short soft prompts and a gating mechanism
to route an input to a specific soft prompt (Choi
et al.
, 2023), and decomposing the soft prompt into
low rank matrices (Shi and Lipani
, 2024).

instances. It also makes the training difficult by

🧠 Big Picture Analogy:
Dynamic Cue Cards for a Stage Actor
Imagine an actor (the LLM) performing in different plays (tasks). Traditionally, you'd give the actor a
static cue card
(a fixed soft prompt) before every scene, regardless of the scene's content. Sometimes it helps, but often it's generic and doesn't match the situation.
ID‑SPAM
is like assigning a
personalized cue card
generated
on the fly
, based on the script for that specific scene (input text). Better cues, better performance — with less effort.
⚙️ Component Analogies:
1.
Self-Attention as a Spotlight Director
Think of the input text as a crowded stage full of actors (tokens). The self-attention mechanism is like a lighting technician who decides
which actors (words) deserve the spotlight
— based on their relevance to the prompt you want to generate. Not all words are equally important; attention highlights the ones that matter most.
🔦
"This phrase about medical symptoms? Highlight it — it'll help us tune the model for a diagnosis task."
2.
MLP as a Prompt Sculptor
After spotlighting key parts of the input, a neural network (MLP) acts like a sculptor who takes that focus and
reshapes it into a custom cue
— a short vector of distilled meaning that can influence the LLM's deeper reasoning.
🧱
"Here's a custom-shaped block based on your input — inject this into the model mid-way and let it steer the interpretation."
3.
Soft Prompt as a Shortcut Key
You can think of the soft prompt as a
hotkey configuration
. Instead of retraining the whole LLM (the entire operating system), you just assign shortcut keys that quickly adapt it for a new domain — like a gaming profile that loads custom macros for different characters.
🎮
"Input suggests this is legal text — here's the 'legal hotkey' with nuances for contract analysis."
⛽ Efficiency Analogy:
Remote Control, Not Rebuilding the Engine
Where full fine-tuning is like taking apart and rebuilding your car's engine for each destination, ID‑SPAM is more like using a
universal remote control
with smart presets that adjust behavior based on where you're headed — without touching the engine.
🏎️
"We don't need to rebuild GPT-2 for sentiment analysis — just give it a 'mood-sensitive prompt' crafted on the fly."
💡 Why It's Smarter than Static Soft Prompts
Static soft prompts are like
prewritten instructions in a book
. Useful, but they can't adapt.
ID‑SPAM's dynamic prompts are like
personalized advice generated in real time by reading your question
— always more relevant, always more nimble.
