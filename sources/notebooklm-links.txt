Paradox of Precaution: AGI Safety and Human Trust
These sources present a complex, unified framework that links thermodynamics, cognition, and ethics, primarily through the Relativistic Scalar–Vector Plenum (RSVP) framework. Several papers discuss the Paradox of Precaution, arguing that efforts to enforce artificial general intelligence (AGI) safety through centralized control and surveillance replicate failure modes by eroding mutual trust and entropy exchange—essential for both human and artificial alignment. Simultaneously, other sources define consciousness as a scale-invariant property of negentropic systems, tracing its continuous evolution from prebiotic chemical networks to institutional self-governance through predictive coding and modularity. Finally, one critique suggests that the apocalyptic AI risk narratives, particularly those from the rationalist community, embody a "psychosis gradient," where fear is disguised as analytic certainty, demonstrating a failure to value the uncertainty necessary for true rationality and open systems.
